\title{BusyLean: Naming Ever Larger Numbers in Lean\\[0.3em]
  \large Research notes on constructive large numbers with formal comparisons}
\author{BusyLean contributors}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We port Cody Roux's \emph{name the biggest number} contest from Coq to Lean~4.
In the contest every number must be a closed, computable definition, and every
claim ``my number is bigger'' must be a kernel-checked theorem. We give an exact
port of the incumbent Contender~5 (the largest numeral of a raw System~T term of
depth~42). We review and rebuild five submitted proposals, and we construct
Contender~6, which combines their best ideas: an object language with W-types
over every finite type, relativized to oracle families, and reflected along
every tree that the language itself can define. Along the way we prove formally
that one proposal's re-implementation of the old language agrees with the exact
port. We then construct Contender~7: System~F with Contender~6 as an oracle.
Its strong normalization is proved inside Lean by Girard's reducibility
candidates, which is possible because Lean's \texttt{Prop} is impredicative,
and normalization runs as a certified total function. Contender~8 adds type
operators (System~F$_\omega$), with candidates lifted to kinds and a sound,
fuel-bounded type checker. Contender~9 reflects
F$_\omega$ along the W-language's trees, and we explain why the Calculus of
Constructions is not a growth-rate jump. Contender~10 makes the jump
cheaply: kind polymorphism lets one program reach kinds of unbounded order
at run time, beyond higher-order arithmetic. A second batch of five proposals is
reviewed; two of its ideas, operator-level reflection and ordinal notations
above a language-defined index, are adopted in Contender~12. Contender~13 adds a predicative universe of
operators, a kind $\mathtt{dyn}$ interpreted as $\sum_k\mathrm{Sem}(k)$, with
level-1 kind variables that range over it. Contender~14 climbs a ladder of
such universes: at input $n$ it reflects over the language with $n$ stacked
universes, all normalized by one theorem that is parametric in the universe.
A fourth batch of proposals contributes a rank hierarchy of transformer lifts,
which Contender~15 applies to the ladder. Contender~16 makes the ladder
transfinite: Lean builds a universe for every well-founded atom order, and
Cantor normal forms give a ladder of length $\omega^\omega$. Contender~17
extends the ladder to length $\varepsilon_0$ by applying the same
well-foundedness lemma hereditarily.
\end{abstract}

\section{The game}

Scott Aaronson's \emph{Who can name the bigger number?} asks for the largest
number one can describe concisely. Cody Roux's repository
\href{https://github.com/codyroux/name-the-biggest-number}{name-the-biggest-number}
adds two caveats that turn the game into one about \emph{proof}. First, numbers
must be \emph{constructive}: computable in principle, with no Busy Beavers.
Second, each new contender must come with a \emph{formal proof} that it beats
the previous one. The original contest uses Coq. Its last entry, Contender~5 by
Samuel Gruetter, is the largest value of a raw STLC+NatRec term (G\"odel's
System~T) of syntax depth at most~42.

\paragraph{The Lean edition.} This repository keeps the contest but switches to
Lean~4 (version 4.34.0). The rules (\texttt{README.md}) are: closed computable
\lean{Nat} definitions; a theorem \lean{contender\_(N-1)\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_N};
the axioms \lean{propext} and \lean{Quot.sound} allowed and
\lean{Classical.choice} forbidden; at most 15\,s to check the definition and
60\,s to check the proof, from a clean build; and no laziness or shenanigans.
The axiom policy is deliberate. \lean{Classical.choice} is the only one of
Lean's three axioms that conflicts with computability, so it is the one that
must go.

\section{How to compare unimaginably large numbers}

All numbers here are far too large to evaluate. The formal theorems compare them
directly, but informal \emph{size} is best discussed with the
\emph{fast-growing hierarchy} $f_\alpha$ indexed by ordinals: $f_0(n)=n+1$,
$f_{\alpha+1}(n)=f_\alpha^{n}(n)$, and $f_\lambda(n)=f_{\lambda[n]}(n)$ at
limits. A total language $L$ whose programs are all provably terminating in a
theory $T$ has its bounded-size maxima growing roughly like $f_{|T|}$, where
$|T|$ is the proof-theoretic ordinal of $T$. Two heuristics organize everything
below.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The base language dominates.} System~T is calibrated by $\varepsilon_0$.
  T plus one type of countably branching trees, with recursion into all finite
  types, is calibrated by the Bachmann--Howard ordinal
  $\BH=\psi(\varepsilon_{\Omega+1})$. With tree classes at every finite level
  the calibration is $\psi(\Omega_\omega)$, the ordinal of $\Pi^1_1$-CA$_0$.
\item \textbf{Reflection multiplies.} Relativizing a language $L$ to an oracle
  that grows like $f_\beta$ yields growth like $f_{\beta+|L|}$. Iterating this
  along an index of height $\gamma$ yields $f_{|L|\cdot\gamma}$. Explicit
  ordinal notations without collapsing functions stay below \BH, so a stack of
  reflections over a weak base cannot catch up with a stronger base.
\end{enumerate}

These are informal calibrations, not theorems; the formal content of every
contender is a strict inequality checked by Lean's kernel.

\section{Contender 5, exactly}

\lean{BusyLean/Contender5.lean} ports \lean{Contender.v} one definition at a
time. The port keeps every quirk that can affect the value: raw ill-typed terms
are interpreted with \lean{error} defaults; variables use reversed de~Bruijn
indices; lambdas carry both annotations and cast their body; arrow casts recurse
only when \lean{type\_eqb} succeeds; the enumeration order is the original one,
including right-nested \lean{++}; and the winner is chosen by \lean{maxBy}'s
strict update, starting from \lean{tO}. A smoke test reproduces the reference
table: $0,2,10,146,24976$ terms and maxima $0,0,1,2,3$ at depths $0$--$4$.

\paragraph{Agreement of an independent port.} Proposal P5 re-implemented the old
language \emph{inside} its larger W-language, with W-aware casts and a plain
maximum instead of \lean{maxBy}, and asserted without proof that the result is
Contender~5. We prove it:
\begin{theorem}[\lean{PortAgreement.largest\_eq\_oldMaximum}]
For every depth $n$,
\[\texttt{C5.largestSTLCNatRecNatOfDepth}\ n \;=\; \texttt{BigNumber.oldMaximum}\ n.\]
\end{theorem}
The proof is a logical relation between the two interpreters: equality at
$\N$, preservation at arrows. That avoids function extensionality. Casts in both
directions preserve the relation, including the error branches. The fundamental
lemma covers reversed lookup, binder casts and natural recursion. P5's
enumeration is the translated image of the original, in the same order, and the
\lean{maxBy} winner attains the maximum.

\section{Contender 6}

\subsection{The object language}
The language is P5's. Types are generated by $A,B::=\N\mid A\to B\mid W\,A$,
where $W\,A$ is the type of well-founded trees branching over~$A$. Terms are raw
(ill-typed terms default), with natural recursion and a W-recursor into every
type. Because $A$ ranges over all types, the language has tree classes of
every finite level, $W\N$, $W(W\N)$, \dots, and its calibration is
$|L|\approx\psi(\Omega_\omega)$.

\subsection{Oracles for free}
P5's interpreter resolves variables by oldest-first levels. Evaluating a program
in the one-element environment $[\langle \N\to\N\to\N, fs\rangle]$ therefore makes
\lean{var 0} denote the oracle family $fs$ everywhere, even under binders. No new
syntax is needed, and the smoke test confirms it: \lean{var0 3 4} evaluates to
$fs(3,4)$, and so does \lean{(fun y => var0 y y) 5} with the family applied at
$(5,5)$.

\subsection{Reflection along the language's own trees}
\begin{lstlisting}
def reflect (fs : Family) (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  n + 1 + max (prefixMax (fun i => fs i n) n)
              (maximum (evalWith fs) (allTerms n))
def stage (seed : Family) : WTree ℕ → ℕ → ℕ
  | .leaf   => reflect seed
  | .node f => reflect (fun i => stage seed (f i))
def treesOfDepth (k : ℕ) : WTree ℕ :=
  .node fun i => defTree (nthD (allTerms k) i .zero)
def index : WTree ℕ := .node treesOfDepth
def contender_6 : ℕ := stage p5Family index 42
\end{lstlisting}
At a node the oracle family \emph{is} the family of child stages, so a program
may call any child at a computed index (P2's forest reflection). The new
ingredient is \lean{index}: its $k$-th child joins every $W\N$ tree denoted by a
program of depth $\le k$. The hierarchy therefore runs along the language's own
ordinals, all of $|L|$, instead of an explicit notation system below \BH. By the
multiplication heuristic, Contender~6 is about $f_{|L|\cdot|L|}(42)$.

\subsection{What is proved}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \lean{contender\_5\_lt\_contender\_6}: the exact port of Contender~5 is below Contender~6.
\item \lean{p5\_lt\_contender\_6} and \lean{p5Family\_lt\_finalFamily}: P5 is beaten at every input.
\item \lean{defined\_tree\_eventually\_lt}: every tree the language defines is eventually dominated.
\item \lean{program\_lt\_finalFamily}: every program of depth $\le n$, run with the final oracles, is exceeded.
\end{itemize}
All of them depend only on \lean{propext}. The definitions check in about 5\,s
and the proofs in about 6\,s. A machine-checked audit shows that the definition
never mentions \lean{contender\_5} or its winner selection. It also reports,
candidly, that P5's seed contains the old language's maximum as a function of
depth.

\section{Engineering lessons}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Toolchain drift changes axiom lists.} In Lean~4.34 a wildcard match
  arm compiles to a \lean{\_sparseCasesOn} helper built on \lean{Nat.land}.
  \lean{Nat.bitwise}'s termination proof uses \lean{propext}, so ``axiom-free on
  4.19'' did not survive the upgrade. The same happens to core \lean{xs[i]?}.
  Exhaustive matches avoid it.
\item \textbf{Tactics carry axioms.} \lean{omega}'s certificates use
  \lean{funext}, and so \lean{Quot.sound}. Two calls were replaced by explicit
  lemma chains.
\item \textbf{Never let the unifier normalize a contender.} Writing
  \lean{iterate f 41 (f 42)} against \lean{iterate f 42 42} made Lean try to
  evaluate the number. Generalizing the statement first, so that only
  $42=41+1$ is checked, fixes it.
\item \textbf{Prove inequalities by inclusion, not monotonicity.} Reflection is
  not monotone in its oracle, because a program may test oracle values. Every
  comparison therefore exhibits an explicit occurrence: a visible oracle index
  or an enumerated program.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contender 7: impredicative polymorphism}

Contender~6 lives below the next collapsing level: everything it does is
predicative. The next qualitative jump is \emph{impredicative polymorphism}. The
numeric functions definable in Girard's System~F on Church numerals are exactly
those provably total in second-order arithmetic $\mathsf{Z}_2$, and
$\psi(\Omega_\omega)$ is merely the ordinal of $\Pi^1_1$-CA$_0$, a small fragment
of $\mathsf{Z}_2$.

\subsection{Why Lean can host System F}
System~F has no set-theoretic model in Lean's predicative \lean{Type}: a model
would have to interpret $\forall X.A$ as a product over \emph{all} types,
including itself. Girard's normalization proof needs much less. It interprets
types as \emph{reducibility candidates}, predicates on terms, and quantifies
over all candidates. In Lean a candidate is a structure whose main field is
$P:\mathrm{Tm}\to\mathrm{Prop}$. A statement ``$\forall C:\mathrm{Cand},\ \dots$''
is again a proposition, because \lean{Prop} is impredicative. The normalization
proof therefore goes through, even though there is no semantic model.

\subsection{The formalization}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Untyped core} (\lean{SystemF/Syntax}, \lean{Reduction}): de~Bruijn
  terms with an oracle constant \lean{orc}; parallel substitution with its
  composition algebra; full $\beta$-reduction plus the $\delta$-rule
  $\mathtt{orc}\,\overline{n}\to\overline{f(n)}$ on canonical Church numerals;
  $\mathrm{SN}$ as accessibility.
\item \textbf{Types} (\lean{Types}): de~Bruijn System~F types and Curry-style
  typing of \emph{erased} terms. A flag enables the oracle rule; numerals are
  typed without it.
\item \textbf{Candidates} (\lean{Candidates}): CR1--CR3, where \emph{neutral}
  means neither a $\lambda$ nor the oracle (\lean{orc a} can reduce at the
  head); the arrow construction; the intersection $\forall$; the interpretation
  $\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho$; its renaming and substitution lemmas, proved as
  \emph{equalities} of candidates via funext, propext and proof irrelevance;
  $\beta$-closure for $\lambda$; and adequacy.
  The oracle is justified afterwards. Numerals lie in every interpretation of
  $\mathtt{nat}$ (adequacy without the oracle rule), so the $\delta$-reduct of
  $\mathtt{orc}\,a$ stays inside the candidate.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{theorem}[\lean{F.sn\_of\_typed}]
Every term typable in System~F with the oracle is strongly normalizing under
$\beta\delta$, for every oracle function $f$. The proof uses only \lean{propext}
and \lean{Quot.sound}.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{From programs to numbers}
Programs are Church-style terms (annotated $\lambda$, explicit type abstraction
and application), so type inference is decidable. \lean{infer\_sound} states
that inference implies the Curry typing of the erased term. A program's value
is computed as follows: check that it is closed of type
$\mathtt{nat}=\forall X.(X\to X)\to X\to X$; erase it; normalize it by the
normal-order step function \lean{nstep}, iterated by well-founded recursion on
the SN certificate (the compiler runs this as an ordinary loop); and read the
Church numeral. A smoke test shows the pipeline computing:
$2^3\mapsto 8$ and $3^{2^2}\mapsto 81$ by polymorphic instantiation, oracle
calls $\mathtt{orc}\,\overline{4}\mapsto 40$ for $f(n)=10n$, and $0$ for
ill-typed programs.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}
def reflectF (f : ℕ → ℕ) (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  n + 1 + max (f n) (maximum (fvalue f) (ctmsUpTo n))
def F7 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := reflectF finalFamily n
def contender_7 : ℕ := F7 (F7 42)
\end{lstlisting}
The oracle is Contender~6's family. System~F programs can call it and amplify it
with second-order strength. The outer \lean{F7} runs System~F at a syntax budget
already larger than Contender~6. Proved:
\lean{contender\_6\_lt\_contender\_7}, \lean{finalFamily\_lt\_F7} (at every
input), and \lean{fvalue\_lt\_contender\_7} (every System~F program of depth
$\le$ \lean{F7 42} is exceeded). The audit confirms that the constant
\lean{contender\_6} itself never occurs in the definition; only Contender~6's
family occurs, as the oracle.

\paragraph{Size.} By the heuristics of Section~2, Contender~7 is about
$f_{|\mathsf{Z}_2|}$ applied at an input beyond Contender~6. Here
$|\mathsf{Z}_2|$ is the proof-theoretic ordinal of second-order arithmetic.
Nobody has computed it, but it lies far beyond every ordinal named in this
article.

\section{Contender 8: type operators}

System~F$_\omega$ adds \emph{type operators}. Kinds are
$\kappa::=\star\mid\kappa\to\kappa$; type constructors are simply typed
$\lambda$-terms over kinds, with $\to$ and $\forall\kappa$ as constants of
kind~$\star$. Programs may now abstract over type operators. A typical example
is $\Lambda F{:}\star\to\star.\,\lambda x{:}F\,\mathtt{nat}.\,x$. The numeric
functions definable in F$_\omega$ are those provably total in higher-order
arithmetic, beyond second-order arithmetic.

\subsection{Candidates over kinds}
The semantic domain is defined by recursion on kinds:
$\mathrm{Sem}(\star)=\mathrm{Cand}$ and
$\mathrm{Sem}(\kappa_1\to\kappa_2)=\mathrm{Sem}(\kappa_1)\to\mathrm{Sem}(\kappa_2)$,
with the arrow on the right being an ordinary Lean function type. A type
operator therefore \emph{denotes a function on candidates}, and type-level
$\beta$-reduction is literally function application. The interpretation
recurses on syntax at an \emph{expected kind}, returning a default element on
kind mismatch. This is the same dynamic-cast idea as the raw interpreters of
Contenders 5 and~6, and it keeps the definition structural. Type application
records its argument's kind for this purpose. The substitution lemma, the
instantiation lemma and the invariance of the interpretation under
$\beta$-conversion are proved for well-kinded types. The beta case of
invariance is one line: function application plus the instantiation lemma.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Fw.sn\_of\_typed}]
Every term typable in System~F$_\omega$ with the oracle is strongly normalizing,
using only \lean{propext} and \lean{Quot.sound}.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Deciding type equality, soundly}
Church-style checking must compare types up to $\beta$. Instead of proving a
second normalization theorem for the type level, the checker compares
\emph{fuel-bounded} normal forms. Every normal-order step is a genuine
$\beta$-step, so equal results witness joinability, and \lean{Fw.infer\_sound}
inserts the corresponding conversions. A program needing more fuel is merely
rejected. That loses programs but never admits an ill-typed one, and
Contender~8 uses the syntax budget as the fuel. The smoke test checks
$(\lambda x{:}(\lambda X{:}\star.X)\,\mathtt{nat}.\,x)\,\overline{3}\mapsto 3$
(rejected with fuel~0), and quantification over an operator,
$(\Lambda F{:}\star{\to}\star.\lambda x{:}F\,\mathtt{nat}.x)\,[\lambda X.X]\,\overline5\mapsto5$.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}
def family7 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := F7 (F7 n)      -- family7 42 = contender_7
def F8 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := reflectFw family7 n
def contender_8 : ℕ := F8 (F8 42)
\end{lstlisting}
A first attempt used \lean{F7} itself as the oracle and tried to compare
\lean{F7 (F7 42)} with \lean{F8 (F8 42)}. That needs monotonicity of
reflection, which fails. Taking Contender~7's whole construction as the
family makes the comparison immediate:
\lean{family7 42 < F8 42 < F8 (F8 42)}.

\section{Contender 9: reflecting \texorpdfstring{F$_\omega$}{F-omega} along the W-language's trees}

\subsection{Why not the Calculus of Constructions}
The natural next rung, the Calculus of Constructions, turns out \emph{not} to
be a growth-rate jump. In pure CoC, term dependencies can be erased into
F$_\omega$ (Geuvers and Nederhof), so the numeric functions CoC represents on
Church numerals are essentially those of F$_\omega$. This is why Loader's number
sits in the F$_\omega$/CoC class, and Contender~8 already lives there. A genuine
jump beyond higher-order arithmetic requires \emph{runtime-to-type dependency
with large elimination}: types computed by recursion on input numbers, so that
a single program uses types of unbounded order. That is the realm of the
Calculus of Inductive Constructions, whose normalization needs a realizability
model with universes. It is a much larger project, described in the roadmap.

\subsection{A multiplicative step}
While that project is pending, Contender~6's amplifier applies to any total
language with an oracle. Contender~9 reflects F$_\omega$ along Contender~6's
self-referential index, the join of every tree the W-language defines:
\begin{lstlisting}
def stage9 (seed : Family) : WTree ℕ → ℕ → ℕ
  | .leaf   => reflect9 seed
  | .node g => reflect9 (fun i => stage9 seed (g i))
def F9 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := stage9 (fun _ => family8) index n
def contender_9 : ℕ := F9 (F9 42)      -- family8 n = F8 (F8 n)
\end{lstlisting}
\lean{reflect9 fs n} exceeds the visible oracle values $fs(i,n)$, $i\le n$, and
every F$_\omega$ program of depth $\le n$. F$_\omega$'s oracle is unary, so the
family is packed as $\mathtt{orc}\,(2^i(2m+1)) = fs(i,m)$. Programs can compute
these codes and so call any child at any input; the theorem
\lean{unpair2\_code} shows that the decoder inverts the code. The index trees
must still come from the W-language: F$_\omega$ has no set-theoretic model, so
its programs cannot hand trees back to Lean. By the multiplication heuristic,
Contender~9 is about $f_{|\mathsf{Z}_\omega|\cdot\psi(\Omega_\omega)^2}$.
Proved: \lean{contender\_8\_lt\_contender\_9}, \lean{family8\_lt\_F9} (at every
input) and \lean{defined\_tree\_eventually\_lt9}.

\subsection{Budgets for a growing stack}
Each contender seeds itself with its predecessor's family, so its definition
transitively imports the whole history. The full chain took 16--30\,s under
parallel load on the reference laptop. A fixed budget for the whole stack would
eventually be exhausted by history alone. The rules now treat earlier accepted
contenders like the precompiled core library: the 15\,s/60\,s budgets apply to
the new contender. Contender~9's definition checks in about 2\,s and its proofs
in about 3\,s, and the full clean build of all contenders takes about 28\,s.

\section{Contender 10: kind polymorphism}

\subsection{Unbounded orders without dependent types}
Each F$_\omega$ program uses finitely many kinds, fixed in its text, and is
provably total in higher-order arithmetic of some finite order. To get past
that, a single program must reach kinds of \emph{unbounded} order, chosen at
run time. Full dependent types with large elimination would do it. A much
cheaper mechanism suffices: types that quantify over \emph{kinds},
$\forall\kappa.A$ (of kind $\star$). A step function
\[g:\ \forall\kappa.\ B(\kappa)\to B(\kappa\to\star),\]
iterated $m$ times through the impredicatively encoded existential
$\exists\kappa.B(\kappa)=\forall Y{:}\star.\,(\forall\kappa.\,B(\kappa)\to Y)\to Y$,
climbs $m$ orders, where $m$ is a run-time number. A totality proof for such
programs needs reducibility candidates for all finite types uniformly, which is
beyond higher-order arithmetic.

\paragraph{Consistency.} In pure type system terms this adds the rule
$(\triangle,\star)$, types quantifying over the sort of kinds, but not
$(\triangle,\Box)$, kinds quantifying over kinds. The latter would give
Girard's System~U$^-$, which is inconsistent by Hurkens' paradox. With only
$(\triangle,\star)$, kinds have no binders, so kind variables always range over
closed kinds, a countable set. The Lean proof below is semantic, so it
certifies normalization, and with it consistency.

\subsection{The model}
A \emph{kind valuation} $\theta$ maps kind variables to closed F$_\omega$ kinds,
and kind annotations are evaluated under~$\theta$. The single new semantic
ingredient is
\[\llbracket\forall\kappa.A\rrbracket_{\theta,\rho} = \bigcap_{K\ \text{closed}}\llbracket A\rrbracket_{K\cdot\theta,\rho},\]
a countable intersection of candidates. The renaming and substitution lemmas
for kind variables hold unconditionally. Adequacy gains two cases: kind
generalization (a fresh kind extends $\theta$, and contexts are kind-shifted)
and kind instantiation (choose the evaluated kind).
\begin{theorem}[\lean{Fk.sn\_of\_typed}]
Every term typable in kind-polymorphic System~F$_\omega$ with the oracle is
strongly normalizing, using only \lean{propext} and \lean{Quot.sound}.
\end{theorem}
The Church-style checker extends Contender~8's with kind abstraction and
application, again proved sound. A smoke test types
$\Lambda\kappa.\Lambda F{:}\kappa.\lambda x{:}\mathtt{nat}.x$ as
$\forall\kappa.\forall F{:}\kappa.\,\mathtt{nat}\to\mathtt{nat}$. It runs it at
kind $\star$ (giving 4) and at kind $\star\to\star$ with the identity operator
(giving 6), and rejects an ill-kinded instantiation.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}
def family9 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := F9 (F9 n)      -- family9 42 = contender_9
def F10 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := reflectFk family9 n
def contender_10 : ℕ := F10 (F10 42)

\section{A second batch of proposals, and Contender 12}

\subsection{Five more proposals}
Five more archives arrived, all proposing a ``Contender~7'' on top of this

\section{Contender 13: a predicative universe of operators}

\subsection{The idea}
Kind polymorphism (Contender~10) lets one program reach every \emph{finite}
order uniformly. The next step is an object that collects all finite orders at
once. Letting kinds quantify over kinds would do it impredicatively, but that
is Girard's System~U$^-$, which is inconsistent. The predicative alternative is
a new kind $\mathtt{dyn}$ whose semantic domain is the dependent sum
\[\mathrm{Sem}(\mathtt{dyn}) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{k\ \text{dyn-free}}\mathrm{Sem}(k),\]
which is exactly the type \lean{SVal} of Contender~8's model. It is a universe
of all finite-order operators that does not contain itself. Two type-level
constructs reach it: $\mathtt{pack}\,K\,A:\mathtt{dyn}$ for $A:K$, and
$\mathtt{unpack}\,K\,X:K$ for $X:\mathtt{dyn}$, with the conversion
$\mathtt{unpack}\,K\,(\mathtt{pack}\,K\,A)\to A$. Both are restricted to
\emph{dyn-free} kinds, a syntactic condition.

Kind variables now come in two sorts. Level-0 variables range over dyn-free
kinds and can appear in $\mathtt{pack}$/$\mathtt{unpack}$. Level-1 variables
range over \emph{all} kinds, including $\mathtt{dyn}$, $\mathtt{dyn}\to\star$,
and so on. The existential climbing trick of Contender~10, applied to level-1
variables, therefore reaches orders $\omega+n$ for run-time~$n$.

\subsection{The model}
Semantic kinds are closed syntactic kinds, which have decidable equality, so
the dynamic read \lean{SVal.get} of Contender~6 still works. A decoding
\lean{toFw} sends dyn-free closed kinds to F$_\omega$ kinds, with
$\mathrm{Sem}(K)=\mathrm{Sem}_{F_\omega}(k)$ whenever
$\mathtt{toFw}\,K=\mathtt{some}\,k$. $\mathtt{pack}$ is \lean{SVal.mk} through
that cast, and $\mathtt{unpack}$ is \lean{SVal.get} through the reverse cast.
The lemma \lean{unpack\_pack} states that the read-back of a packed value is
the value itself, which is what makes the new conversion sound. Level-0 kind
variables are evaluated to F$_\omega$ kinds and level-1 variables to arbitrary
kinds; $\forall\kappa_0$ and $\forall\kappa_1$ are intersections over the
respective ranges.
\begin{theorem}[\lean{Fd.sn\_of\_typed}]
Every term typable in dynamic-kind System~F$_\omega$ with the oracle is
strongly normalizing, using only \lean{propext} and \lean{Quot.sound}.
\end{theorem}
The smoke test exercises all of it. A program typable only through
$\mathtt{unpack}\,\star\,(\mathtt{pack}\,\star\,\mathtt{nat})\equiv\mathtt{nat}$
evaluates to 3. Level-1 polymorphism instantiated at $\mathtt{dyn}$ evaluates
to 5. Instantiating a level-0 variable with $\mathtt{dyn}$, and packing at a
kind containing $\mathtt{dyn}$, are both rejected.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}
def family12 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := F12 (F12 n)    -- family12 42 = contender_12
def F13 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := reflectFd family12 n
def contender_13 : ℕ := F13 (F13 42)
\end{lstlisting}
Proved: \lean{contender\_12\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_13},
\lean{family12\_lt\_F13} at every input, and
\lean{fvalue\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_13}. The new definition modules check
in about 7.5\,s.

against Contender~6). Three compile completely (P11, P14, P15). The other three
fail in their proofs, all through the same trap: a proof step that makes Lean

\section{Contender 14: the ladder of universes}

\subsection{The idea}
$\mathtt{dyn}$ collects the operators of every dyn-free kind, but it never
contains itself, and nothing collects operators whose kinds mention
$\mathtt{dyn}$. The predicative answer is a ladder. The level-$\ell$ language
has atomic kinds $\mathtt{dyn}_0,\dots,\mathtt{dyn}_{\ell-1}$, and atom
$\mathtt{dyn}_i$ denotes the $\mathtt{dyn}$ of the level-$i$ language. Two
type-level constructs move operators into and out of an atom:
\[
\frac{A:K\qquad K\ \text{fits}\ a}{\mathtt{apack}\,a\,K\,A : a}
\qquad
\frac{X:a\qquad K\ \text{fits}\ a}{\mathtt{aunpack}\,a\,K\,X : K}
\qquad
\mathtt{aunpack}\,a\,K\,(\mathtt{apack}\,a\,K\,A)\ \longrightarrow\ A,
\]
where $K$ \emph{fits} $a$ when $K$ is built from $\star$, $\to$ and atoms
strictly older than~$a$. The top-level $\mathtt{dyn}$ and both sorts of kind
variables stay as in Contender~13. So a program can pack an operator of kind
$\mathtt{dyn}_0\to\star$ into $\mathtt{dyn}_1$ and read it back.

\subsection{One normalization proof for every level}
Writing a new model for each level would never finish, so the language and
its model are \emph{parametric}. The syntax (\lean{SystemFL}) is parametric in
a type $\alpha$ of atoms with decidable equality and an order
(\lean{AtomOrder}). The model takes a \lean{Universe}: a domain
$\mathrm{El}(a)$ for each atom, a packing map from the base kinds that fit~$a$,
a reading map, and the law
\[\mathrm{rd}_a\,k\,(\mathrm{pk}_a\,k\,v) = v .\]
Base kinds are $\star$, $\to$ and atoms, interpreted with
$\mathrm{Sem}(a)=\mathrm{El}(a)$. $\mathtt{dyn}$ is the sum over base kinds,
and $\mathtt{apack}$/$\mathtt{aunpack}$ are $\mathrm{pk}$/$\mathrm{rd}$ through
the decoding cast. The read-back law is exactly what the new conversion needs.
\begin{theorem}[\lean{FL.sn\_of\_typed}]
For every universe $U$, every term typable in the ladder language over $U$'s
atoms (with the oracle) is strongly normalizing.
\end{theorem}
The ladder itself (\lean{SystemFL/Ladder.lean}) instantiates this theorem at
every level:
\begin{itemize}
\item atoms are $\mathtt{Atom}\,0=\mathtt{Empty}$ and
  $\mathtt{Atom}\,(\ell+1)=\mathtt{Option}\,(\mathtt{Atom}\,\ell)$. The new
  atom $\mathtt{none}$ lies above all older atoms $\mathtt{some}\,a$;
\item \lean{nextUniv} interprets the new atom as \lean{BVal} of the previous
  universe, i.e.\ as its $\mathtt{dyn}$, and keeps the older atoms;
\item packing and reading go through \lean{lower}, which recognizes the
  level-$(\ell+1)$ base kinds that avoid the new atom and turns them into
  level-$\ell$ base kinds. Semantic domains agree along it, so the read-back
  law at level $\ell+1$ reduces to the law at level~$\ell$.
\end{itemize}
One engineering point matters. $\mathrm{pk}$ receives a proof that the kind
fits, so the lowered kind is extracted with \lean{Option.get} rather than a
dependent \lean{match}. The law then needs no rewriting under a dependent
match, the usual source of ill-typed motives.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}
def ladderUpTo (n : ℕ) := ctmsUpTo (Atom.all n) n     -- level-n programs
def reflectFL (f : ℕ → ℕ) (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  n + 1 + maxN (f n) (maximum (fvalue n f n) (ladderUpTo n))
def family13 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := F13 (F13 n)    -- family13 42 = contender_13
def F14 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := reflectFL family13 n
def contender_14 : ℕ := F14 (F14 42)
\end{lstlisting}
The value of a level-$\ell$ program is computed by the certified normalizer
with the SN proof \lean{FL.sn\_of\_typed} applied to the level-$\ell$ universe
\lean{Univ f}. The reflection
diagonalizes \emph{at the meta level}. Input $n$ uses the level-$n$ language,
so no fixed rung keeps up with $F_{14}$. Proved:
\lean{contender\_13\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_14},
\lean{family13\_lt\_F14} at every input, and
\lean{fvalue\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_14}. The smoke test packs
$\lambda\_{:}\mathtt{dyn}_0.\,\mathtt{nat}$ into $\mathtt{dyn}_1$ and applies its
read-back to a packed $\mathtt{nat}$, which evaluates to~4. It also confirms that
an atom cannot hold its own kind.

The first budget run hit the 15\,s definition limit (16.0\,s on a loaded
machine). Profiling showed the kind checker's soundness proof taking 2.4\,s,
with $6\times6$ case splits inside nested matches. Factoring the checker into
small result functions (\lean{arrK}, \lean{appK}, \lean{apackK}, \dots) with
standalone inversion lemmas cut that module from 5.8\,s to 3.2\,s. The
definitions now check in about 10\,s.

\section{A fourth batch of proposals, and Contender 15}

\subsection{Four proposals over Contender 13}
Four more proposals (P17--P20) arrived while Contender~14 was being built.
All of them target Contender~13. All keep its language, dynamic-kind
F$_\omega$, and make its reflection \emph{family-aware}: programs receive a
packed countable oracle family, and a prefix maximum over the visible
coordinates makes the transformer progressive without any monotonicity.
Three compile as submitted (P17, P19, and P20, whose package says Lean was
never run). P18 fails at a single proof step. A \lean{simpa} misses the
definitional equality
$\mathtt{operatorStage}\,b\,\mathtt{leaf}=\mathtt{liftTransformer}\,b$, and
replacing it with \lean{exact} repairs it. All audits pass with
\lean{propext} and \lean{Quot.sound}.

Writing $\tau$ for the autonomous schedule above \lean{tree12}, P18 and P19
stack operator stages into towers of height $\mathtt{family13}\,n$ or $n+1$,
about $|F_d|\cdot\tau^\omega$. P17 and P20 independently found something
stronger, a \emph{rank hierarchy of transformer lifts}:
\[
\mathrm{lift}_0 = \mathtt{liftTransformer},\qquad
\mathrm{lift}_{k+1}(b) = \mathrm{stage}_{\mathrm{lift}_k}\bigl(\mathrm{lift}_k(b),\ \sigma\bigr),
\]
where $\mathrm{stage}_L(b,\sigma)$ traverses the schedule $\sigma$, applying
the lift $L$ at every node. Rank $k+1$ uses the whole rank-$k$ operator at
every node, so each rank roughly exponentiates the index. Diagonalized at rank
$\mathtt{family13}\,n+1$, this gives about $|F_d|\cdot\varepsilon(\tau)$. It
strictly generalizes Contender~12's single operator stage. All four exceed
Contender~13 (each proves it), but by the base-language heuristic all four lie
below the ladder of Contender~14.

\subsection{Contender 15}
The two routes compose. The lift hierarchy is generic over transformers, so it
can be applied to the ladder's reflection instead of F$_d$'s. Contender~15
adopts P17's modules unchanged, with attribution, and defines:
\begin{lstlisting}
def reflectFLFamily (fs : Family) (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  n + 1 + maxN (prefixMax (fun i => fs i n) n)
    (maximum (FL.fvalue n (packFamily fs) n) (FL.ladderUpTo n))

Every proof follows an explicit occurrence: of the seed in the prefix

\section{Contender 16: a transfinite ladder}

\subsection{The length of the ladder is the lever}
Contender~14 diagonalizes over finitely many universes. Letting a program
choose its level at run time would add only about one more factor of $\omega$.
A much larger lever is the \emph{length} of the ladder. The ladder language
was parametric in its atoms from the start, and only the \lean{Option} tower of
Contender~14 was finite. So the question becomes: for which atom orders can
Lean build a universe?

\begin{theorem}[\lean{FL.wfUniv}]
For every type of atoms whose decidable order is well-founded, there is a
universe. Hence (\lean{FL.sn\_of\_typed}) the ladder language over those atoms is
strongly normalizing.
\end{theorem}
The domain of atom $a$ is defined by well-founded recursion
(\lean{WellFounded.fix}):
\[
\mathrm{El}(a) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{k\ \text{fits}\ a}\ \mathrm{Sem}(k),
\qquad \mathrm{Sem}(b) = \mathrm{El}(b)\ \text{for atoms } b<a .
\]
A value stores its kind together with a proof that the kind fits. Reading
compares kinds, which is decidable, so the law $\mathrm{rd}(\mathrm{pk}\,v)=v$
holds. The unfolding equation \lean{WellFounded.fix\_eq} is not
definitional, so it is used through casts. Because types are erased,
\lean{WellFounded.fix} at \lean{Type} compiles without a
\lean{noncomputable} marker. The universe at a limit atom such as $\omega$
holds operators from \emph{every} lower level at once.

\subsection{Well-founded Cantor normal forms}
A non-increasing list $[a_1,\dots,a_k]$ denotes
$\omega^{a_1}+\dots+\omega^{a_k}$, and the lexicographic order on such lists is
the ordinal order. \lean{Ordinal.wf\_sorted} proves this order well-founded
whenever the exponents are accessible. The proof uses neither transitivity nor
trichotomy; asymmetry comes from accessibility. A sorted list headed by $h$
splits into a run $h^{k+1}$ and a tail below $h$. Two such lists compare like
the pairs $(k,\text{tail})$, which gives an induction on the head, then on the
run length, then on the tail. With natural-number exponents this gives
notations below $\omega^\omega$ (\lean{OmegaPow}). Applied hereditarily it will
give $\varepsilon_0$.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}
def transfiniteUpTo (n : ℕ) := ctmsUpTo (omegaPowUpTo n) n   -- atoms: ≤ n terms, exponents < n
def family15 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := F15 (F15 n)
def F16 (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  diagonalLift (schedule16 n) (family15 n + 1) tTransformer (fun _ => family15) n n
def contender_16 : ℕ := F16 (F16 42)
\end{lstlisting}
\lean{tTransformer} is family-aware reflection over the transfinite ladder,
normalized by \lean{sn\_of\_typed (wfUniv f omegaPow\_wf)}. At depth~$n$,
programs reach levels up to $\omega^{n-1}\cdot n$. Proved:
\lean{contender\_15\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_16}, \lean{family15\_lt\_F16} and
\lean{tvalue\_lt\_F16}. The smoke test packs an operator of kind
$\mathtt{dyn}_3\to\star$ into the level-$\omega$ universe and evaluates it to~4.
The opposite direction is rejected. The definitions check in 4.8\,s and the
proofs in 2.8\,s.

\section{Contender 17: a ladder of length \texorpdfstring{$\varepsilon_0$}{epsilon0}}

Notations below $\varepsilon_0$ are trees $\mathtt{node}\,[a_1,\dots,a_k]$
denoting $\omega^{a_1}+\dots+\omega^{a_k}$. They are compared
lexicographically, child by child, and a tree is normal when its children are
normal and non-increasing. On arbitrary trees the lexicographic order is
\emph{not} well-founded. For example, $t_{k+1}=\mathtt{node}\,[0, t_k]$
descends forever starting from $t_1=\omega$. So the sorted-list lemma cannot be
applied to all trees.

The fix is to build normality into the Boolean element order,
$\mathrm{lt}_P(a,b) := \mathrm{nf}(a)\wedge \mathrm{lt}(a,b)$, and apply the
generic lemma \emph{to that order}. Two short lemmas then finish the
proof:
\begin{itemize}
\item \lean{acc\_of\_children}: if the list of children of $t$ is accessible
  (sorted-lexicographically), then $t$ is accessible. A smaller normal tree has
  a smaller sorted list of children;
\item \lean{acc\_nf}: every normal tree is accessible. By structural
  recursion its \emph{head} child is accessible, so \lean{acc\_head} makes the
  list of children accessible. The other children lie below the head and need
  no recursion.
\end{itemize}
\lean{Ordinal.epsilon0\_wf} transfers this to the subtype of normal forms, and
\lean{FL.wfUniv} supplies the universes unchanged. At depth $n$, programs use
atoms of height $\le n$, i.e.\ levels up to towers $\omega^{\omega^{\cdots}}$ of
height about~$n$. The contender has the same shape as before,
\lean{F17 n := diagonalLift (schedule17 n) (family16 n + 1) eTransformer (fun \_ => family16) n n}.
Proved: \lean{contender\_16\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_17}, \lean{family16\_lt\_F17}
and \lean{evalue\_lt\_F17}. The smoke test packs an operator over level
$\omega$ into the level-$\omega^\omega$ universe (value~5), and the
opposite direction is rejected. The definitions check in 2.0\,s and the proofs
in 1.9\,s.

\section{Roadmap}

\paragraph{Longer ladders.} The ladder now has whatever length Lean can
prove well-founded, and $\varepsilon_0$ is done. Beyond that, collapsing-function notations ($\psi(\Omega_\nu)$
and further) are well-founded in Lean's type theory, and each one plugs into
\lean{wfUniv} unchanged.

\paragraph{Reflection along trees.} As with Contenders~9 and~11, reflecting
the ladder along the W-language's trees is a cheap multiplicative step.

\paragraph{Large elimination and trees.} Runtime-to-type dependency with large
elimination remains the route to a CIC-like system. A language able to hand
trees back to Lean would unlock the world and model constructions of P8--P10.
