\begin{abstract}
We port Cody Roux's \emph{name the biggest number} contest from Coq to Lean~4.
In the contest every number must be a closed, computable definition, and every
claim ``my number is bigger'' must be a kernel-checked theorem. We give an exact
port of the incumbent Contender~5 (the largest numeral of a raw System~T term of
depth~42). We review and rebuild five submitted proposals, and we construct
Contender~6, which combines their best ideas: an object language with W-types
over every finite type, relativized to oracle families, and reflected along
every tree that the language itself can define. Along the way we prove formally
that one proposal's re-implementation of the old language agrees with the exact
port. We then construct Contender~7: System~F with Contender~6 as an oracle.
Its strong normalization is proved inside Lean by Girard's reducibility
candidates, which is possible because Lean's \texttt{Prop} is impredicative,
and normalization runs as a certified total function. Contender~8 adds type
operators (System~F$_\omega$), with candidates lifted to kinds and a sound,
fuel-bounded type checker. Contender~9 reflects
F$_\omega$ along the W-language's trees, and we explain why the Calculus of
Constructions is not a growth-rate jump. Contender~10 makes the jump
cheaply: kind polymorphism lets one program reach kinds of unbounded order
at run time, beyond higher-order arithmetic. A second batch of five proposals is
reviewed; two of its ideas, operator-level reflection and ordinal notations
above a language-defined index, are adopted in Contender~12. Contender~13 adds a predicative universe of
operators, a kind $\mathtt{dyn}$ interpreted as $\sum_k\mathrm{Sem}(k)$, with
level-1 kind variables that range over it. Contender~14 climbs a ladder of
such universes: at input $n$ it reflects over the language with $n$ stacked
universes, all normalized by one theorem that is parametric in the universe.
A fourth batch of proposals contributes a rank hierarchy of transformer lifts,
which Contender~15 applies to the ladder. Contender~16 makes the ladder
transfinite: Lean builds a universe for every well-founded atom order, and
Cantor normal forms give a ladder of length $\omega^\omega$. Contender~17
extends the ladder to length $\varepsilon_0$ by applying the same
well-foundedness lemma hereditarily, and Contender~18 to length $\Gamma_0$ with a
Schütte-style proof for binary Veblen terms that follows only the comparison's
case split. Contender~19 extends the same technique to Schütte's Klammersymbole,
whose argument positions are themselves terms, giving a ladder of
large-Veblen length. Contender~20 reaches the Bachmann--Howard ordinal with a
Buchholz-style collapsing notation, whose well-foundedness proof is the
classical impredicative argument carried by Lean's \lean{Acc}. Contender~21
bounds the number of collapsing levels, which turns the multi-level argument
into a downward induction over relativized scopes. It then diagonalizes over the
bound, so ladder lengths climb to Buchholz's ordinal $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$.
Contender~22 relativizes the bounded systems to a well-founded base and
iterates, which goes beyond $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$. Contender~23 adds a
proposal's transfinite-rank hierarchy of lifts, and Contender~24 iterates the
towers along well-founded indices, each round indexed by the previous one.
Contender~25 adopts a proposal's key idea: atoms are addresses in a Lean-defined
tree, so well-foundedness is structural and free. The ladder then runs over
trees built by Buchholz-style collapsing functions defined by plain structural
recursion, heuristically to $\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega+n})$.
Contender~26 indexes the collapsing levels by tree ordinals themselves,
heuristically climbing toward the fixed points of $\alpha\mapsto\psi_0(\Omega_\alpha)$.
Contender~27 stratifies level codes into universes, toward an inaccessible-like
level, and Contender~28 stacks the universes along tree ordinals given by
earlier collapses. Contender~29 iterates the universe-building operators
themselves, indexed by tree ordinals, and Contender~30 lifts closure to all
finite types over universes; Contender~31 to transfinite types indexed by
collapsed trees. Contender~32 turns the constructions into programs of a typed
language over tree ordinals with a semantic interpreter, and takes all programs of
bounded size. Contender~33 stacks the language on itself, and Contender~34 stacks
it along tree ordinals. Contender~35 takes its trees from impredicative System~F
programs, made well-founded by a reducibility candidate for trees. Contender~36
lets the ladder language build the trees, so each ladder defines the length of
the next. Contender~37 stacks the tree languages over this jump. A correction
(Section~\ref{sec:truncation}): a ladder program of depth $n$ sees only a finite
truncation of its atom order, so Contenders~15--37 grew through their
amplifiers, not through the size of their atoms. Contender~38 runs a

\section{The game}

Scott Aaronson's \emph{Who can name the bigger number?} asks for the largest
number one can describe concisely. Cody Roux's repository
\href{https://github.com/codyroux/name-the-biggest-number}{name-the-biggest-number}
adds two caveats that turn the game into one about \emph{proof}. First, numbers
must be \emph{constructive}: computable in principle, with no Busy Beavers.
Second, each new contender must come with a \emph{formal proof} that it beats
the previous one. The original contest uses Coq. Its last entry, Contender~5 by
Samuel Gruetter, is the largest value of a raw STLC+NatRec term (G\"odel's
System~T) of syntax depth at most~42.

\paragraph{The Lean edition.} This repository keeps the contest but switches to
Lean~4 (version 4.34.0). The rules (\texttt{README.md}) are: closed computable
\lean{Nat} definitions; a theorem \lean{contender\_(N-1)\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_N};
the axioms \lean{propext} and \lean{Quot.sound} allowed and
\lean{Classical.choice} forbidden; at most 15\,s to check the definition and
60\,s to check the proof, from a clean build; and no laziness or shenanigans.
Contenders 6--70 were accepted under a rule that allowed the previous
contender's construction as an oracle or seed. That rule has been withdrawn:
a new contender may not use any smaller one in its definition, and the
contest continues with the independent tower of
Section~\ref{sec:independent}. The axiom policy is deliberate. \lean{Classical.choice} is the only one of
Lean's three axioms that conflicts with computability, so it is the one that
must go.

\section{How to compare unimaginably large numbers}

All numbers here are far too large to evaluate. The formal theorems compare them
directly, but informal \emph{size} is best discussed with the
\emph{fast-growing hierarchy} $f_\alpha$ indexed by ordinals: $f_0(n)=n+1$,
$f_{\alpha+1}(n)=f_\alpha^{n}(n)$, and $f_\lambda(n)=f_{\lambda[n]}(n)$ at
limits. A total language $L$ whose programs are all provably terminating in a
theory $T$ has its bounded-size maxima growing roughly like $f_{|T|}$, where
$|T|$ is the proof-theoretic ordinal of $T$. Two heuristics organize everything
below.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The base language dominates.} System~T is calibrated by $\varepsilon_0$.
  T plus one type of countably branching trees, with recursion into all finite
  types, is calibrated by the Bachmann--Howard ordinal
  $\BH=\psi(\varepsilon_{\Omega+1})$. With tree classes at every finite level
  the calibration is $\psi(\Omega_\omega)$, the ordinal of $\Pi^1_1$-CA$_0$.
\item \textbf{Reflection multiplies.} Relativizing a language $L$ to an oracle
  that grows like $f_\beta$ yields growth like $f_{\beta+|L|}$. Iterating this
  along an index of height $\gamma$ yields $f_{|L|\cdot\gamma}$. Explicit
  ordinal notations without collapsing functions stay below \BH, so a stack of
  reflections over a weak base cannot catch up with a stronger base.
\end{enumerate}

These are informal calibrations, not theorems; the formal content of every
contender is a strict inequality checked by Lean's kernel.

\section{Contender 5, exactly}

\lean{BusyLean/Contender5.lean} ports \lean{Contender.v} one definition at a
time. The port keeps every quirk that can affect the value: raw ill-typed terms
are interpreted with \lean{error} defaults; variables use reversed de~Bruijn
indices; lambdas carry both annotations and cast their body; arrow casts recurse
only when \lean{type\_eqb} succeeds; the enumeration order is the original one,
including right-nested \lean{++}; and the winner is chosen by \lean{maxBy}'s
strict update, starting from \lean{tO}. A smoke test reproduces the reference
table: $0,2,10,146,24976$ terms and maxima $0,0,1,2,3$ at depths $0$--$4$.

\paragraph{Agreement of an independent port.} Proposal P5 re-implemented the old
language \emph{inside} its larger W-language, with W-aware casts and a plain
maximum instead of \lean{maxBy}, and asserted without proof that the result is
Contender~5. We prove it:
\begin{theorem}[\lean{PortAgreement.largest\_eq\_oldMaximum}]
For every depth $n$,
\[\texttt{C5.largestSTLCNatRecNatOfDepth}\ n \;=\; \texttt{BigNumber.oldMaximum}\ n.\]
\end{theorem}
The proof is a logical relation between the two interpreters: equality at
$\N$, preservation at arrows. That avoids function extensionality. Casts in both
directions preserve the relation, including the error branches. The fundamental
lemma covers reversed lookup, binder casts and natural recursion. P5's
enumeration is the translated image of the original, in the same order, and the
\lean{maxBy} winner attains the maximum.

Five proposals arrived as archives; \lean{proposals/REVIEW.md} has the details.
All were rebuilt on Lean~4.34.0, and the two that had never been compiled (P4,

\section{Contender 6}

\subsection{The object language}
The language is P5's. Types are generated by $A,B::=\N\mid A\to B\mid W\,A$,
where $W\,A$ is the type of well-founded trees branching over~$A$. Terms are raw
(ill-typed terms default), with natural recursion and a W-recursor into every
type. Because $A$ ranges over all types, the language has tree classes of
every finite level, $W\N$, $W(W\N)$, \dots, and its calibration is
$|L|\approx\psi(\Omega_\omega)$.

\subsection{Oracles for free}
P5's interpreter resolves variables by oldest-first levels. Evaluating a program
in the one-element environment $[\langle \N\to\N\to\N, fs\rangle]$ therefore makes
\lean{var 0} denote the oracle family $fs$ everywhere, even under binders. No new
syntax is needed, and the smoke test confirms it: \lean{var0 3 4} evaluates to
$fs(3,4)$, and so does \lean{(fun y => var0 y y) 5} with the family applied at
$(5,5)$.

\subsection{Reflection along the language's own trees}
\begin{lstlisting}
def reflect (fs : Family) (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  n + 1 + max (prefixMax (fun i => fs i n) n)
              (maximum (evalWith fs) (allTerms n))
def stage (seed : Family) : WTree ℕ → ℕ → ℕ
  | .leaf   => reflect seed
  | .node f => reflect (fun i => stage seed (f i))
def treesOfDepth (k : ℕ) : WTree ℕ :=
  .node fun i => defTree (nthD (allTerms k) i .zero)
def index : WTree ℕ := .node treesOfDepth
def contender_6 : ℕ := stage p5Family index 42
\end{lstlisting}
At a node the oracle family \emph{is} the family of child stages, so a program
may call any child at a computed index (P2's forest reflection). The new
ingredient is \lean{index}: its $k$-th child joins every $W\N$ tree denoted by a
program of depth $\le k$. The hierarchy therefore runs along the language's own
ordinals, all of $|L|$, instead of an explicit notation system below \BH. By the
multiplication heuristic, Contender~6 is about $f_{|L|\cdot|L|}(42)$.

\subsection{What is proved}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \lean{contender\_5\_lt\_contender\_6}: the exact port of Contender~5 is below Contender~6.
\item \lean{p5\_lt\_contender\_6} and \lean{p5Family\_lt\_finalFamily}: P5 is beaten at every input.
\item \lean{defined\_tree\_eventually\_lt}: every tree the language defines is eventually dominated.
\item \lean{program\_lt\_finalFamily}: every program of depth $\le n$, run with the final oracles, is exceeded.
\end{itemize}
All of them depend only on \lean{propext}. The definitions check in about 5\,s
and the proofs in about 6\,s. A machine-checked audit shows that the definition
never mentions \lean{contender\_5} or its winner selection. It also reports,
candidly, that P5's seed contains the old language's maximum as a function of
depth.

\section{Engineering lessons}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Toolchain drift changes axiom lists.} In Lean~4.34 a wildcard match
  arm compiles to a \lean{\_sparseCasesOn} helper built on \lean{Nat.land}.
  \lean{Nat.bitwise}'s termination proof uses \lean{propext}, so ``axiom-free on
  4.19'' did not survive the upgrade. The same happens to core \lean{xs[i]?}.
  Exhaustive matches avoid it.
\item \textbf{Tactics carry axioms.} \lean{omega}'s certificates use
  \lean{funext}, and so \lean{Quot.sound}. Two calls were replaced by explicit
  lemma chains.
\item \textbf{Never let the unifier normalize a contender.} Writing
  \lean{iterate f 41 (f 42)} against \lean{iterate f 42 42} made Lean try to
  evaluate the number. Generalizing the statement first, so that only
  $42=41+1$ is checked, fixes it.
\item \textbf{Prove inequalities by inclusion, not monotonicity.} Reflection is
  not monotone in its oracle, because a program may test oracle values. Every
  comparison therefore exhibits an explicit occurrence: a visible oracle index
  or an enumerated program.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contender 7: impredicative polymorphism}

Contender~6 lives below the next collapsing level: everything it does is
predicative. The next qualitative jump is \emph{impredicative polymorphism}. The
numeric functions definable in Girard's System~F on Church numerals are exactly
those provably total in second-order arithmetic $\mathsf{Z}_2$, and
$\psi(\Omega_\omega)$ is merely the ordinal of $\Pi^1_1$-CA$_0$, a small fragment
of $\mathsf{Z}_2$.

\subsection{Why Lean can host System F}
System~F has no set-theoretic model in Lean's predicative \lean{Type}: a model
would have to interpret $\forall X.A$ as a product over \emph{all} types,
including itself. Girard's normalization proof needs much less. It interprets
types as \emph{reducibility candidates}, predicates on terms, and quantifies
over all candidates. In Lean a candidate is a structure whose main field is
$P:\mathrm{Tm}\to\mathrm{Prop}$. A statement ``$\forall C:\mathrm{Cand},\ \dots$''
is again a proposition, because \lean{Prop} is impredicative. The normalization
proof therefore goes through, even though there is no semantic model.

\subsection{The formalization}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Untyped core} (\lean{SystemF/Syntax}, \lean{Reduction}): de~Bruijn
  terms with an oracle constant \lean{orc}; parallel substitution with its
  composition algebra; full $\beta$-reduction plus the $\delta$-rule
  $\mathtt{orc}\,\overline{n}\to\overline{f(n)}$ on canonical Church numerals;
  $\mathrm{SN}$ as accessibility.
\item \textbf{Types} (\lean{Types}): de~Bruijn System~F types and Curry-style
  typing of \emph{erased} terms. A flag enables the oracle rule; numerals are
  typed without it.
\item \textbf{Candidates} (\lean{Candidates}): CR1--CR3, where \emph{neutral}
  means neither a $\lambda$ nor the oracle (\lean{orc a} can reduce at the
  head); the arrow construction; the intersection $\forall$; the interpretation
  $\llbracket A\rrbracket_\rho$; its renaming and substitution lemmas, proved as
  \emph{equalities} of candidates via funext, propext and proof irrelevance;
  $\beta$-closure for $\lambda$; and adequacy.
  The oracle is justified afterwards. Numerals lie in every interpretation of
  $\mathtt{nat}$ (adequacy without the oracle rule), so the $\delta$-reduct of
  $\mathtt{orc}\,a$ stays inside the candidate.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{theorem}[\lean{F.sn\_of\_typed}]
Every term typable in System~F with the oracle is strongly normalizing under
$\beta\delta$, for every oracle function $f$. The proof uses only \lean{propext}
and \lean{Quot.sound}.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{From programs to numbers}
Programs are Church-style terms (annotated $\lambda$, explicit type abstraction
and application), so type inference is decidable. \lean{infer\_sound} states
that inference implies the Curry typing of the erased term. A program's value
is computed as follows: check that it is closed of type
$\mathtt{nat}=\forall X.(X\to X)\to X\to X$; erase it; normalize it by the
normal-order step function \lean{nstep}, iterated by well-founded recursion on
the SN certificate (the compiler runs this as an ordinary loop); and read the
Church numeral. A smoke test shows the pipeline computing:
$2^3\mapsto 8$ and $3^{2^2}\mapsto 81$ by polymorphic instantiation, oracle
calls $\mathtt{orc}\,\overline{4}\mapsto 40$ for $f(n)=10n$, and $0$ for
ill-typed programs.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}
def reflectF (f : ℕ → ℕ) (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  n + 1 + max (f n) (maximum (fvalue f) (ctmsUpTo n))
def F7 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := reflectF finalFamily n
def contender_7 : ℕ := F7 (F7 42)
\end{lstlisting}
The oracle is Contender~6's family. System~F programs can call it and amplify it
with second-order strength. The outer \lean{F7} runs System~F at a syntax budget
already larger than Contender~6. Proved:
\lean{contender\_6\_lt\_contender\_7}, \lean{finalFamily\_lt\_F7} (at every
input), and \lean{fvalue\_lt\_contender\_7} (every System~F program of depth
$\le$ \lean{F7 42} is exceeded). The audit confirms that the constant
\lean{contender\_6} itself never occurs in the definition; only Contender~6's
family occurs, as the oracle.

\paragraph{Size.} By the heuristics of Section~2, Contender~7 is about
$f_{|\mathsf{Z}_2|}$ applied at an input beyond Contender~6. Here
$|\mathsf{Z}_2|$ is the proof-theoretic ordinal of second-order arithmetic.
Nobody has computed it, but it lies far beyond every ordinal named in this
article.

\section{Contender 8: type operators}

System~F$_\omega$ adds \emph{type operators}. Kinds are
$\kappa::=\star\mid\kappa\to\kappa$; type constructors are simply typed
$\lambda$-terms over kinds, with $\to$ and $\forall\kappa$ as constants of
kind~$\star$. Programs may now abstract over type operators. A typical example
is $\Lambda F{:}\star\to\star.\,\lambda x{:}F\,\mathtt{nat}.\,x$. The numeric
functions definable in F$_\omega$ are those provably total in higher-order
arithmetic, beyond second-order arithmetic.

\subsection{Candidates over kinds}
The semantic domain is defined by recursion on kinds:
$\mathrm{Sem}(\star)=\mathrm{Cand}$ and
$\mathrm{Sem}(\kappa_1\to\kappa_2)=\mathrm{Sem}(\kappa_1)\to\mathrm{Sem}(\kappa_2)$,
with the arrow on the right being an ordinary Lean function type. A type
operator therefore \emph{denotes a function on candidates}, and type-level
$\beta$-reduction is literally function application. The interpretation
recurses on syntax at an \emph{expected kind}, returning a default element on
kind mismatch. This is the same dynamic-cast idea as the raw interpreters of
Contenders 5 and~6, and it keeps the definition structural. Type application
records its argument's kind for this purpose. The substitution lemma, the
instantiation lemma and the invariance of the interpretation under
$\beta$-conversion are proved for well-kinded types. The beta case of
invariance is one line: function application plus the instantiation lemma.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Fw.sn\_of\_typed}]
Every term typable in System~F$_\omega$ with the oracle is strongly normalizing,
using only \lean{propext} and \lean{Quot.sound}.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Deciding type equality, soundly}
Church-style checking must compare types up to $\beta$. Instead of proving a
second normalization theorem for the type level, the checker compares
\emph{fuel-bounded} normal forms. Every normal-order step is a genuine
$\beta$-step, so equal results witness joinability, and \lean{Fw.infer\_sound}
inserts the corresponding conversions. A program needing more fuel is merely
rejected. That loses programs but never admits an ill-typed one, and
Contender~8 uses the syntax budget as the fuel. The smoke test checks
$(\lambda x{:}(\lambda X{:}\star.X)\,\mathtt{nat}.\,x)\,\overline{3}\mapsto 3$
(rejected with fuel~0), and quantification over an operator,
$(\Lambda F{:}\star{\to}\star.\lambda x{:}F\,\mathtt{nat}.x)\,[\lambda X.X]\,\overline5\mapsto5$.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}
def family7 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := F7 (F7 n)      -- family7 42 = contender_7
def F8 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := reflectFw family7 n
def contender_8 : ℕ := F8 (F8 42)
\end{lstlisting}
A first attempt used \lean{F7} itself as the oracle and tried to compare
\lean{F7 (F7 42)} with \lean{F8 (F8 42)}. That needs monotonicity of
reflection, which fails. Taking Contender~7's whole construction as the
family makes the comparison immediate:
\lean{family7 42 < F8 42 < F8 (F8 42)}.

\section{Contender 9: reflecting \texorpdfstring{F$_\omega$}{F-omega} along the W-language's trees}

\subsection{Why not the Calculus of Constructions}
The natural next rung, the Calculus of Constructions, turns out \emph{not} to
be a growth-rate jump. In pure CoC, term dependencies can be erased into
F$_\omega$ (Geuvers and Nederhof), so the numeric functions CoC represents on
Church numerals are essentially those of F$_\omega$. This is why Loader's number
sits in the F$_\omega$/CoC class, and Contender~8 already lives there. A genuine
jump beyond higher-order arithmetic requires \emph{runtime-to-type dependency
with large elimination}: types computed by recursion on input numbers, so that
a single program uses types of unbounded order. That is the realm of the
Calculus of Inductive Constructions, whose normalization needs a realizability
model with universes. It is a much larger project, described in the roadmap.

\subsection{A multiplicative step}
While that project is pending, Contender~6's amplifier applies to any total
language with an oracle. Contender~9 reflects F$_\omega$ along Contender~6's
self-referential index, the join of every tree the W-language defines:

\section{Contender 10: kind polymorphism}

\subsection{Unbounded orders without dependent types}
Each F$_\omega$ program uses finitely many kinds, fixed in its text, and is
provably total in higher-order arithmetic of some finite order. To get past
that, a single program must reach kinds of \emph{unbounded} order, chosen at
run time. Full dependent types with large elimination would do it. A much
cheaper mechanism suffices: types that quantify over \emph{kinds},
$\forall\kappa.A$ (of kind $\star$). A step function
\[g:\ \forall\kappa.\ B(\kappa)\to B(\kappa\to\star),\]
iterated $m$ times through the impredicatively encoded existential
$\exists\kappa.B(\kappa)=\forall Y{:}\star.\,(\forall\kappa.\,B(\kappa)\to Y)\to Y$,
climbs $m$ orders, where $m$ is a run-time number. A totality proof for such
programs needs reducibility candidates for all finite types uniformly, which is
beyond higher-order arithmetic.

\paragraph{Consistency.} In pure type system terms this adds the rule
$(\triangle,\star)$, types quantifying over the sort of kinds, but not
$(\triangle,\Box)$, kinds quantifying over kinds. The latter would give
Girard's System~U$^-$, which is inconsistent by Hurkens' paradox. With only
$(\triangle,\star)$, kinds have no binders, so kind variables always range over
closed kinds, a countable set. The Lean proof below is semantic, so it
certifies normalization, and with it consistency.

\subsection{The model}
A \emph{kind valuation} $\theta$ maps kind variables to closed F$_\omega$ kinds,
and kind annotations are evaluated under~$\theta$. The single new semantic
ingredient is
\[\llbracket\forall\kappa.A\rrbracket_{\theta,\rho} = \bigcap_{K\ \text{closed}}\llbracket A\rrbracket_{K\cdot\theta,\rho},\]
a countable intersection of candidates. The renaming and substitution lemmas
for kind variables hold unconditionally. Adequacy gains two cases: kind
generalization (a fresh kind extends $\theta$, and contexts are kind-shifted)
and kind instantiation (choose the evaluated kind).
\begin{theorem}[\lean{Fk.sn\_of\_typed}]
Every term typable in kind-polymorphic System~F$_\omega$ with the oracle is
strongly normalizing, using only \lean{propext} and \lean{Quot.sound}.
\end{theorem}
The Church-style checker extends Contender~8's with kind abstraction and
application, again proved sound. A smoke test types
$\Lambda\kappa.\Lambda F{:}\kappa.\lambda x{:}\mathtt{nat}.x$ as
$\forall\kappa.\forall F{:}\kappa.\,\mathtt{nat}\to\mathtt{nat}$. It runs it at
kind $\star$ (giving 4) and at kind $\star\to\star$ with the identity operator
(giving 6), and rejects an ill-kinded instantiation.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}
def family9 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := F9 (F9 n)      -- family9 42 = contender_9
def F10 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := reflectFk family9 n
def contender_10 : ℕ := F10 (F10 42)
\end{lstlisting}
Proved: \lean{contender\_9\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_10}, \lean{family9\_lt\_F10} (at
every input), and \lean{fvalue\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_10}. The new definition modules,
four for the language plus the contender, check in about 12\,s.

\section{Contender 13: a predicative universe of operators}

\subsection{The idea}
Kind polymorphism (Contender~10) lets one program reach every \emph{finite}
order uniformly. The next step is an object that collects all finite orders at
once. Letting kinds quantify over kinds would do it impredicatively, but that
is Girard's System~U$^-$, which is inconsistent. The predicative alternative is
a new kind $\mathtt{dyn}$ whose semantic domain is the dependent sum
\[\mathrm{Sem}(\mathtt{dyn}) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{k\ \text{dyn-free}}\mathrm{Sem}(k),\]
which is exactly the type \lean{SVal} of Contender~8's model. It is a universe
of all finite-order operators that does not contain itself. Two type-level
constructs reach it: $\mathtt{pack}\,K\,A:\mathtt{dyn}$ for $A:K$, and
$\mathtt{unpack}\,K\,X:K$ for $X:\mathtt{dyn}$, with the conversion
$\mathtt{unpack}\,K\,(\mathtt{pack}\,K\,A)\to A$. Both are restricted to
\emph{dyn-free} kinds, a syntactic condition.

Kind variables now come in two sorts. Level-0 variables range over dyn-free
kinds and can appear in $\mathtt{pack}$/$\mathtt{unpack}$. Level-1 variables
range over \emph{all} kinds, including $\mathtt{dyn}$, $\mathtt{dyn}\to\star$,
and so on. The existential climbing trick of Contender~10, applied to level-1
variables, therefore reaches orders $\omega+n$ for run-time~$n$.

\subsection{The model}
Semantic kinds are closed syntactic kinds, which have decidable equality, so
the dynamic read \lean{SVal.get} of Contender~6 still works. A decoding
\lean{toFw} sends dyn-free closed kinds to F$_\omega$ kinds, with
$\mathrm{Sem}(K)=\mathrm{Sem}_{F_\omega}(k)$ whenever
$\mathtt{toFw}\,K=\mathtt{some}\,k$. $\mathtt{pack}$ is \lean{SVal.mk} through
that cast, and $\mathtt{unpack}$ is \lean{SVal.get} through the reverse cast.
The lemma \lean{unpack\_pack} states that the read-back of a packed value is
the value itself, which is what makes the new conversion sound. Level-0 kind
variables are evaluated to F$_\omega$ kinds and level-1 variables to arbitrary
kinds; $\forall\kappa_0$ and $\forall\kappa_1$ are intersections over the
respective ranges.
\begin{theorem}[\lean{Fd.sn\_of\_typed}]
Every term typable in dynamic-kind System~F$_\omega$ with the oracle is
strongly normalizing, using only \lean{propext} and \lean{Quot.sound}.
\end{theorem}
The smoke test exercises all of it. A program typable only through
$\mathtt{unpack}\,\star\,(\mathtt{pack}\,\star\,\mathtt{nat})\equiv\mathtt{nat}$
evaluates to 3. Level-1 polymorphism instantiated at $\mathtt{dyn}$ evaluates
to 5. Instantiating a level-0 variable with $\mathtt{dyn}$, and packing at a
kind containing $\mathtt{dyn}$, are both rejected.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}

def F13 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := reflectFd family12 n
def contender_13 : ℕ := F13 (F13 42)
\end{lstlisting}
Proved: \lean{contender\_12\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_13},
\lean{family12\_lt\_F13} at every input, and
\lean{fvalue\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_13}. The new definition modules check
in about 7.5\,s.

\section{Contender 14: the ladder of universes}

\subsection{The idea}
$\mathtt{dyn}$ collects the operators of every dyn-free kind, but it never
contains itself, and nothing collects operators whose kinds mention
$\mathtt{dyn}$. The predicative answer is a ladder. The level-$\ell$ language
has atomic kinds $\mathtt{dyn}_0,\dots,\mathtt{dyn}_{\ell-1}$, and atom
$\mathtt{dyn}_i$ denotes the $\mathtt{dyn}$ of the level-$i$ language. Two
type-level constructs move operators into and out of an atom:
\[
\frac{A:K\qquad K\ \text{fits}\ a}{\mathtt{apack}\,a\,K\,A : a}
\qquad
\frac{X:a\qquad K\ \text{fits}\ a}{\mathtt{aunpack}\,a\,K\,X : K}
\qquad
\mathtt{aunpack}\,a\,K\,(\mathtt{apack}\,a\,K\,A)\ \longrightarrow\ A,
\]
where $K$ \emph{fits} $a$ when $K$ is built from $\star$, $\to$ and atoms
strictly older than~$a$. The top-level $\mathtt{dyn}$ and both sorts of kind
variables stay as in Contender~13. So a program can pack an operator of kind
$\mathtt{dyn}_0\to\star$ into $\mathtt{dyn}_1$ and read it back.

\subsection{One normalization proof for every level}
Writing a new model for each level would never finish, so the language and
its model are \emph{parametric}. The syntax (\lean{SystemFL}) is parametric in
a type $\alpha$ of atoms with decidable equality and an order
(\lean{AtomOrder}). The model takes a \lean{Universe}: a domain
$\mathrm{El}(a)$ for each atom, a packing map from the base kinds that fit~$a$,
a reading map, and the law
\[\mathrm{rd}_a\,k\,(\mathrm{pk}_a\,k\,v) = v .\]
Base kinds are $\star$, $\to$ and atoms, interpreted with
$\mathrm{Sem}(a)=\mathrm{El}(a)$. $\mathtt{dyn}$ is the sum over base kinds,
and $\mathtt{apack}$/$\mathtt{aunpack}$ are $\mathrm{pk}$/$\mathrm{rd}$ through
the decoding cast. The read-back law is exactly what the new conversion needs.
\begin{theorem}[\lean{FL.sn\_of\_typed}]
For every universe $U$, every term typable in the ladder language over $U$'s
atoms (with the oracle) is strongly normalizing.
\end{theorem}
The ladder itself (\lean{SystemFL/Ladder.lean}) instantiates this theorem at
every level:
\begin{itemize}
\item atoms are $\mathtt{Atom}\,0=\mathtt{Empty}$ and
  $\mathtt{Atom}\,(\ell+1)=\mathtt{Option}\,(\mathtt{Atom}\,\ell)$. The new
  atom $\mathtt{none}$ lies above all older atoms $\mathtt{some}\,a$;
\item \lean{nextUniv} interprets the new atom as \lean{BVal} of the previous
  universe, i.e.\ as its $\mathtt{dyn}$, and keeps the older atoms;
\item packing and reading go through \lean{lower}, which recognizes the
  level-$(\ell+1)$ base kinds that avoid the new atom and turns them into
  level-$\ell$ base kinds. Semantic domains agree along it, so the read-back
  law at level $\ell+1$ reduces to the law at level~$\ell$.
\end{itemize}
One engineering point matters. $\mathrm{pk}$ receives a proof that the kind
fits, so the lowered kind is extracted with \lean{Option.get} rather than a
dependent \lean{match}. The law then needs no rewriting under a dependent
match, the usual source of ill-typed motives.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}
def ladderUpTo (n : ℕ) := ctmsUpTo (Atom.all n) n     -- level-n programs
def reflectFL (f : ℕ → ℕ) (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  n + 1 + maxN (f n) (maximum (fvalue n f n) (ladderUpTo n))
def family13 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := F13 (F13 n)    -- family13 42 = contender_13
def F14 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := reflectFL family13 n
def contender_14 : ℕ := F14 (F14 42)
\end{lstlisting}
The value of a level-$\ell$ program is computed by the certified normalizer
with the SN proof \lean{FL.sn\_of\_typed} applied to the level-$\ell$ universe
\lean{Univ f}. The reflection
diagonalizes \emph{at the meta level}. Input $n$ uses the level-$n$ language,
so no fixed rung keeps up with $F_{14}$. Proved:
\lean{contender\_13\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_14},
\lean{family13\_lt\_F14} at every input, and
\lean{fvalue\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_14}. The smoke test packs
$\lambda\_{:}\mathtt{dyn}_0.\,\mathtt{nat}$ into $\mathtt{dyn}_1$ and applies its
read-back to a packed $\mathtt{nat}$, which evaluates to~4. It also confirms that
an atom cannot hold its own kind.

The first budget run hit the 15\,s definition limit (16.0\,s on a loaded
machine). Profiling showed the kind checker's soundness proof taking 2.4\,s,
with $6\times6$ case splits inside nested matches. Factoring the checker into
small result functions (\lean{arrK}, \lean{appK}, \lean{apackK}, \dots) with
standalone inversion lemmas cut that module from 5.8\,s to 3.2\,s. The
definitions now check in about 10\,s.

\section{A fourth batch of proposals, and Contender 15}

\subsection{Four proposals over Contender 13}
Four more proposals (P17--P20) arrived while Contender~14 was being built.
All of them target Contender~13. All keep its language, dynamic-kind

packed countable oracle family, and a prefix maximum over the visible
coordinates makes the transformer progressive without any monotonicity.
Three compile as submitted (P17, P19, and P20, whose package says Lean was

\section{Contender 16: a transfinite ladder}

\subsection{The length of the ladder is the lever}
Contender~14 diagonalizes over finitely many universes. Letting a program
choose its level at run time would add only about one more factor of $\omega$.
A much larger lever is the \emph{length} of the ladder. The ladder language
was parametric in its atoms from the start, and only the \lean{Option} tower of
Contender~14 was finite. So the question becomes: for which atom orders can
Lean build a universe?

\begin{theorem}[\lean{FL.wfUniv}]
For every type of atoms whose decidable order is well-founded, there is a
universe. Hence (\lean{FL.sn\_of\_typed}) the ladder language over those atoms is
strongly normalizing.
\end{theorem}
The domain of atom $a$ is defined by well-founded recursion
(\lean{WellFounded.fix}):
\[
\mathrm{El}(a) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{k\ \text{fits}\ a}\ \mathrm{Sem}(k),
\qquad \mathrm{Sem}(b) = \mathrm{El}(b)\ \text{for atoms } b<a .
\]
A value stores its kind together with a proof that the kind fits. Reading
compares kinds, which is decidable, so the law $\mathrm{rd}(\mathrm{pk}\,v)=v$
holds. The unfolding equation \lean{WellFounded.fix\_eq} is not
definitional, so it is used through casts. Because types are erased,
\lean{WellFounded.fix} at \lean{Type} compiles without a
\lean{noncomputable} marker. The universe at a limit atom such as $\omega$
holds operators from \emph{every} lower level at once.

\subsection{Well-founded Cantor normal forms}
A non-increasing list $[a_1,\dots,a_k]$ denotes
$\omega^{a_1}+\dots+\omega^{a_k}$, and the lexicographic order on such lists is
the ordinal order. \lean{Ordinal.wf\_sorted} proves this order well-founded
whenever the exponents are accessible. The proof uses neither transitivity nor
trichotomy; asymmetry comes from accessibility. A sorted list headed by $h$
splits into a run $h^{k+1}$ and a tail below $h$. Two such lists compare like
the pairs $(k,\text{tail})$, which gives an induction on the head, then on the
run length, then on the tail. With natural-number exponents this gives
notations below $\omega^\omega$ (\lean{OmegaPow}). Applied hereditarily it will
give $\varepsilon_0$.

\subsection{The contender}
\begin{lstlisting}
def transfiniteUpTo (n : ℕ) := ctmsUpTo (omegaPowUpTo n) n   -- atoms: ≤ n terms, exponents < n
def family15 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := F15 (F15 n)
def F16 (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  diagonalLift (schedule16 n) (family15 n + 1) tTransformer (fun _ => family15) n n
def contender_16 : ℕ := F16 (F16 42)
\end{lstlisting}
\lean{tTransformer} is family-aware reflection over the transfinite ladder,
normalized by \lean{sn\_of\_typed (wfUniv f omegaPow\_wf)}. At depth~$n$,
programs reach levels up to $\omega^{n-1}\cdot n$. Proved:
\lean{contender\_15\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_16}, \lean{family15\_lt\_F16} and
\lean{tvalue\_lt\_F16}. The smoke test packs an operator of kind
$\mathtt{dyn}_3\to\star$ into the level-$\omega$ universe and evaluates it to~4.
The opposite direction is rejected. The definitions check in 4.8\,s and the
proofs in 2.8\,s.

\section{Contender 17: a ladder of length \texorpdfstring{$\varepsilon_0$}{epsilon0}}

Notations below $\varepsilon_0$ are trees $\mathtt{node}\,[a_1,\dots,a_k]$
denoting $\omega^{a_1}+\dots+\omega^{a_k}$. They are compared
lexicographically, child by child, and a tree is normal when its children are
normal and non-increasing. On arbitrary trees the lexicographic order is
\emph{not} well-founded. For example, $t_{k+1}=\mathtt{node}\,[0, t_k]$
descends forever starting from $t_1=\omega$. So the sorted-list lemma cannot be
applied to all trees.

The fix is to build normality into the Boolean element order,
$\mathrm{lt}_P(a,b) := \mathrm{nf}(a)\wedge \mathrm{lt}(a,b)$, and apply the
generic lemma \emph{to that order}. Two short lemmas then finish the
proof:
\begin{itemize}
\item \lean{acc\_of\_children}: if the list of children of $t$ is accessible
  (sorted-lexicographically), then $t$ is accessible. A smaller normal tree has
  a smaller sorted list of children;
\item \lean{acc\_nf}: every normal tree is accessible. By structural
  recursion its \emph{head} child is accessible, so \lean{acc\_head} makes the
  list of children accessible. The other children lie below the head and need
  no recursion.
\end{itemize}
\lean{Ordinal.epsilon0\_wf} transfers this to the subtype of normal forms, and
\lean{FL.wfUniv} supplies the universes unchanged. At depth $n$, programs use
atoms of height $\le n$, i.e.\ levels up to towers $\omega^{\omega^{\cdots}}$ of
height about~$n$. The contender has the same shape as before,
\lean{F17 n := diagonalLift (schedule17 n) (family16 n + 1) eTransformer (fun \_ => family16) n n}.
Proved: \lean{contender\_16\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_17}, \lean{family16\_lt\_F17}
and \lean{evalue\_lt\_F17}. The smoke test packs an operator over level
$\omega$ into the level-$\omega^\omega$ universe (value~5), and the
opposite direction is rejected. The definitions check in 2.0\,s and the proofs
in 1.9\,s.

\section{Contender 18: a ladder of length \texorpdfstring{$\Gamma_0$}{Gamma0}}

\subsection{Veblen terms and a case-split proof}
Terms are sums $\mathtt{node}\,[\varphi(a_1,b_1),\dots,\varphi(a_k,b_k)]$ of
principal terms (a mutual, nested inductive). Sums are compared
lexicographically, and principal terms by Veblen's rule. The rule is
implemented as a Boolean function by mutual well-founded recursion on sizes:
\begin{lstlisting}
def ltP : P → P → Bool
  | .phi a b, .phi c d =>
      if ltV a c then ltVP b c d          -- b < phi(c,d)
      else if decide (a = c) then ltV b d
      else leVP a b d                     -- phi(a,b) <= d
\end{lstlisting}
A term is normal when every sum is non-increasing, hereditarily. No
fixed-point condition is imposed, so non-canonical terms such as
$\varphi(0,\varepsilon_0)$ are allowed. They simply sit somewhere in the
order. The genuine Veblen normal forms are among the normal terms, so the order
type is at least $\Gamma_0$.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Veblen.acc\_phi}, \lean{gamma0\_wf}]
If $a$ and $b$ are accessible, so is $\varphi(a,b)$. Hence every normal term
is accessible, and the order on normal terms is well-founded.
\end{theorem}
The proof is Schütte's, reorganized so that it only follows the
\emph{definitional} case split of \lean{ltP}. It inducts on $a$, then on $b$,
then on the size of a predecessor $\varphi(e,f)$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $e<a$ and $f<\varphi(a,b)$. Then $f$ is $0$ or headed by a smaller
  principal term below $\varphi(a,b)$, accessible by the size induction, so
  \lean{acc\_head} makes $f$ accessible. The induction on $a$ applies to $e$;
\item $e=a$ and $f<b$: by the induction on $b$;
\item otherwise $\varphi(e,f)\le b$: then the one-term sum $\varphi(e,f)$ is
  below or equal to $b$, hence accessible (\lean{accP\_of\_le}).
\end{itemize}
Transitivity, trichotomy and ordinal arithmetic are never needed. Sums are
handled by \lean{SortedLex} as for $\varepsilon_0$.

One engineering note. The first version depended on
\lean{Classical.choice}. \lean{omega} had been called on a goal of the form
\lean{AccP q}, after deriving an arithmetic contradiction. Because that goal is not decidable, \lean{omega}
used \lean{Classical.byContradiction}. Writing \lean{absurd hqs (by omega)}
gives \lean{omega} a decidable goal, and the axioms return to \lean{propext} and
\lean{Quot.sound}.

\section{Contender 19: a ladder of large-Veblen length}

\subsection{Klammersymbole}
Binary Veblen generalizes to $\varphi$ applied to an \emph{argument array}: a
finite list of entries (position, value), sorted by decreasing position. In
Schütte's Klammersymbole the positions are themselves terms. Terms are now a
three-way mutual inductive (sums, principal terms $\varphi(A)$, entries).
Arrays are compared lexicographically by entries, with entries ordered by
position and then value. This is exactly the order "first difference at the
most significant position". Principal terms follow the generalized rule:
\[
\varphi(A)<\varphi(B)\iff
\begin{cases}
A<B \text{ and every value of } A \text{ is } <\varphi(B), \text{ or}\\
A\ne B,\ A\not<B, \text{ and } \varphi(A)\le \text{some value of } B.
\end{cases}
\]
Binary Veblen is the case of positions $0$ and $1$: $\varphi(1@1)=\varepsilon_0$
and $\varphi(1@2)=\Gamma_0$. With positions ranging over the notation itself,
the normal forms reach the large Veblen ordinal.

\subsection{Well-foundedness}
The proof still follows only the comparison's case split. Two new ingredients
are needed:
\begin{itemize}
\item \lean{SortedLexQ}: the sorted-list lemma restricted to a Prop-valued
  predicate. An entry is \emph{good} when it is normal and its position and
  value are accessible. Good entries are accessible (\lean{accE}: induction on
  the position, then the value). Hence sorted arrays of good entries are
  accessible (\lean{accQ\_sorted}).
\item \lean{posAcc}: if $\Gamma<A$ as sorted arrays, every position of
  $\Gamma$ lies below or at a position of $A$, so it is accessible when
  $A$'s positions are.
\end{itemize}
\lean{LVeblen.acc\_phi} inducts on the array, then on the size of the
predecessor $\varphi(\Gamma)$. In the first case the values of $\Gamma$ are
below $\varphi(A)$ and accessible by the size induction, and the positions by
\lean{posAcc}. So $\Gamma$ is a smaller good array and the array induction
applies. In the second case $\varphi(\Gamma)$ lies below a value of $A$. The
whole module checks in about 2\,s, and the axioms are \lean{propext} and
\lean{Quot.sound}.

\section{Contender 20: a ladder of Bachmann--Howard length}

\subsection{Collapsing, and why the case split no longer suffices}
Buchholz-style terms with two levels are sums of principal terms $D_0a$
($\psi_0(a)$, countable) and $D_1a$ ($\psi_1(a)$, at least $\Omega$). Principal
terms are compared lexicographically (level, then argument), and sums
lexicographically, so every $D_1$ term lies above every $D_0$ term. This raw
order is not well-founded:
\[D_1 0 \;>\; D_0D_1 0 \;>\; D_0D_0D_10 \;>\;\cdots\]
Normal forms exclude this. $D_u a$ is normal only when every element of
$G_u(a)$ is below~$a$, where $G_u(D_v b)=\emptyset$ for $v<u$ and
$\{b\}\cup G_u(b)$ otherwise. In the chain, $D_0D_10$ is normal, but
$D_0D_0D_10$ is not, because $D_10\in G_0(D_0D_10)$ is not below $D_0D_10$. The
normal forms reach $\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega+1})$, the Bachmann--Howard
ordinal.

\subsection{The impredicative argument in Lean}
Let $M$ be the set of terms whose \emph{top-level $D_0$-components} (reached
through sums and $D_1$ only) are accessible.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \lean{accM\_V} (Claim 1): the order restricted to $M$ is well-founded.
  This part is predicative. $D_0$ components are accessible by assumption,
  $D_1$ terms by induction on their argument, and sums by \lean{SortedLexQ}.
\item \lean{accD0} (Claim 2): if $a$ is accessible within $M$, then $D_0a$ is
  accessible. A predecessor $D_0c$ has $c<a$. Its components $D_0f$ satisfy
  $f<c$ by normality, hence $f<a$ by \emph{transitivity}, and they are
  smaller, so an inner size induction puts $c$ in $M$. The outer induction then
  applies to $c$.
\item Claim 3: every normal $D_0c$ is accessible (size induction plus Claims 1
  and~2). $D_1b$ is accessible whenever $b$ is, because its predecessors are
  $D_0$ terms or $D_1c$ with $c<b$. Hence every normal term is accessible
  (\lean{bachmannHoward\_wf}).
\end{enumerate}
This is the point where the well-foundedness proof itself becomes
impredicative. Claim~1 is an accessibility statement \emph{relative to} a set
defined from accessibility, and Lean's inductive \lean{Acc} carries it without
any extra axiom. Transitivity, avoided until now, is needed in Claim~2. The order
is purely lexicographic and structurally recursive, so transitivity is a short
mutual induction. \lean{SortedLexQ} gained \lean{accQ\_head\_of\_acc}, which
starts from one accessible head rather than a global hypothesis. The module
checks in about 2\,s.

\section{Contender 21: Buchholz ladders up to \texorpdfstring{$\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$}{psi0(Omega omega)}}

\subsection{Why more levels are hard}
With levels $0,1,2,\dots$ the two-level argument of Contender~20 regresses.
The order restricted to $M_0$ contains every $D_1d$ with $d\in M_0$ arbitrary,
and every $D_2b$ lies above all of them. So Claim~1 at level~0 needs a level-1
collapse \emph{relative to} $M_0$, which needs a level-2 collapse relative to a
smaller scope, and so on. There is no bound, because high levels hide inside
low arguments: $\psi_0(\Omega_9)<\Omega_1$. The literature resolves this with
Buchholz's distinguished sets.

\subsection{A level bound turns it into a downward induction}
For a fixed bound $L$ on all levels, the regress stops. A \emph{scope} is a
predicate $Z$ on principal terms, and the order is restricted to normal
in-scope terms. For $u\le L+1$ let
\begin{quote}
$R(u)$: if every in-scope term of level ${<}\,u$ is accessible and scopes are
closed under arguments at levels $\ge u$, then every in-scope normal principal
term is accessible.
\end{quote}
$R(L+1)$ holds because every level is below $L+1$. \lean{Buch.stepR} derives
$R(u)$ from $R(u+1)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Restrict the scope to $Z'$, the in-scope terms whose top-level
  $u$-components (through sums and higher levels) are accessible. $R(u+1)$
  applied to $Z'$ makes the $Z'$-order well-founded (Claim~1).
\item Induction along it, an inner size induction and transitivity make
  $D_ua$ accessible for every $a$ accessible within $Z'$ (Claim~2).
\item Every in-scope $D_uc$ is then accessible (Claim~3).
\item $R(u+1)$ applied to $Z$ itself gives the conclusion.
\end{enumerate}
$R(0)$ for the full scope gives \lean{buchholz\_wf L}: the notation with levels
$\le L$ is well-founded. Its normal forms (for $D_ua$: $f<a$ for every top-level
$u$-component $D_uf$ of $a$) include Buchholz's, so the order type is at least
$\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_L+1})$. Relations are written as \lean{LTQ (scope
$\wedge$ nf) ltP}, so \lean{SortedLexQ} and \lean{Subrelation.accessible} do
all the bookkeeping between scopes.

\lean{wfUniv f (buchholz\_wf n)}. The ladder lengths therefore climb through

\section{Contender 22: a tower of relativized Buchholz notations}

\subsection{The barrier at \texorpdfstring{$\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$}{psi0(Omega omega)}}
Contender~21's argument needs a bound on the levels: the downward induction
starts at $R(L+1)$. A single notation for all levels has no such start, and
the relativized argument regresses forever. The literature crosses this barrier
with Buchholz's \emph{distinguished sets}: sets $X$ such that, for each
$\alpha\in X$, $X$ agrees below the next regular above~$\alpha$ with the
well-founded part of a closure $C^\alpha(X)$. The maximal distinguished class is
then shown to contain every notation. Arai's lecture notes present the
technique for reflection notations. Porting it means formalizing a large part of
the notation theory (closure sets, their monotonicity, and the comparison
facts), so it remains on the roadmap.

\subsection{Relativizing to a base}
A cheaper step stays within finitely nested accessibility. The level-bounded
systems are relativized to a type of \emph{base atoms} with a transitive,
well-founded order (\lean{BuchholzBase.lean}). Atoms $B\,b$ lie below every
collapse but may occur inside collapse arguments, so each collapse ranges over
everything below it. The well-foundedness proof is Contender~21's
\lean{stepR}. Only the treatment of atoms changes: they are accessible in every
scope by the base's own well-foundedness (\lean{accB}), not by the level
induction.

The union of all level-bounded systems over a base, ordered by the bound
\emph{first}, is again a transitive, well-founded, decidable order. It is
well-founded by induction on the bound and then \lean{acc\_nf}. So it can serve
as the base of the next round:
\begin{lstlisting}
structure WFO where           -- order, transitivity, well-foundedness, enumeration
  T : Type
  ...
def tower : ℕ → WFO
  | 0 => WFO.empty
  | k + 1 => (tower k).next     -- all bounded systems over tower k
\end{lstlisting}
\lean{tower 1} has order type $\psi_0(\Omega_\omega)$. Each further step
collapses over everything below it, heuristically giving
$\psi_0(\Omega_\omega\cdot k)$ for \lean{tower k}.

def towerAlong (J : WFO) (j : J.T) : WFO :=

def limitAlong (J : WFO) : WFO := sigma J (towerAlong J)

\section{Contender 25: well-foundedness for free}

\subsection{More proposals, and one decisive idea}
Four more proposals arrived (P23--P26), all over Contender~19's language. P23
and P26 raise the lift hierarchy one type level (lifts of lifts). P24
rediscovers P22's W-tree-ranked hierarchy. They all compile, P23 after the
same one-step \lean{simpa} repair as P18, and none exceeds the current
contenders. P25 contributes the decisive idea. Its atoms are \emph{finite
addresses in a computable W-tree}. Following an address is a computation, so
validity is decidable. The order between addresses is well-founded \emph{by
structural recursion on the tree}, whatever ordinal the tree denotes.

All ladders since Contender~16 paid for their length with a hand-written
well-foundedness proof, and that cost stopped the notations at Buchholz's
ordinal. With addresses, the cost disappears. The ladder length is the rank of
\emph{any} tree that Lean can define, and Lean defines trees by structural
recursion, with no proof obligations.

\subsection{Addresses}
\lean{TreeAddress.lean} uses tree ordinals (zero, successor, countable limits,
and suprema over two index types, used by collapsing) and the \emph{strict
descendant} order on valid addresses. P25 used immediate children. Accessibility
of an address follows by recursion on the subtree it reaches
(\lean{acc\_follow}). A strict descendant of $a$ is a descendant of some child
of $a$ (\lean{isDesc\_split}), and addresses below a dead end are invalid.
\lean{addr\_wf} uses only \lean{propext}.

\subsection{Collapsing functions by structural recursion}
\lean{TreeCollapse.lean} defines Buchholz-style collapsing on tree ordinals for
all finite levels at once. Level-$(w{+}1)$ trees have suprema over all lower
levels (lumped in one index type) and over level~$w$. The argument space has
suprema at every finite level. Then
\begin{lstlisting}
def D : (v : ℕ) → TopT → LevT v
  | v, .zero => LevT.Omega v
  | v, .succ t => LevT.epsNext v (D v t)
  | v, .lim f => LevT.lim v fun n => D v (f n)
  | v, .supH g =>        -- below v: keep the supremum; for every j >= v: diagonalize
      ... D v (g (v + i, seq (v + i) n)) ...   -- seq j (n+1) = D j (g (j, seq j n))
\end{lstlisting}
Lean accepts this as structural recursion: every recursive call is on
\lean{g x} for some~\lean{x}. \lean{D} depends on no axioms at all.
The countable tree \lean{bigTree n} collapses $n{+}1$ further $\varepsilon$-numbers
above $\Omega_\omega+1$, where $\Omega_\omega=\lim_k\Omega_k$. Heuristically
its rank is $\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega+n})$. That is beyond Buchholz's
ordinal, and in exactly the range where a notation-based proof would need
distinguished sets.

\section{Contender 26: transfinitely many levels}

Contender~25's collapsing functions have finitely many levels. With tree
ordinals, the levels can themselves be countable tree ordinals
(\lean{TreeLevels.lean}). The type of everything below level $a$ is defined by
structural recursion on the level tree:
\[
\mathrm{Low}(0)=\emptyset,\qquad
\mathrm{Low}(a{+}1)=\mathrm{Low}(a)\oplus\mathrm{Lev}(a),\qquad
\mathrm{Low}(\lim f)=\textstyle\sum_i \mathrm{Low}(f\,i)\oplus\mathrm{Lev}(f\,i),
\]
with $\mathrm{Lev}(a)$ the trees with suprema over $\mathrm{Low}(a)$. Embedding
a level into a higher one extends suprema by zero, so no retraction is needed.

A difficulty arises for arguments. Splitting a supremum into the parts below
and above the target level would need a decidable order on levels, and tree
orders are not decidable. The fix is to let suprema carry their level. The
argument type has a constructor \lean{supAt (c : CTree) (g : Low c → LTop)},
which is still strictly positive. Then
\begin{lstlisting}
def DL : (a : CTree) → LTop → Lev a
  | a, .supAt c g =>
      match c, g with
      | .succ c', g => -- x0 = Omega c',  x(k+1) = DL c' (g x(k));  result lim_k DL a (g x(k))
      | .lim f, g   => -- the limit over i of the successor case at f i
      ...
\end{lstlisting}
Again every recursive call is on \lean{g x}, so the definition is structural,
and it uses only \lean{propext}. \lean{ladderTree n} iterates
$\Lambda\mapsto \mathrm{DL}\,0\,(\varepsilon_{\Omega_\Lambda+1})$ $n{+}1$ times,
starting from Contender~25's tree. Heuristically this climbs toward the fixed
points of $\alpha\mapsto\psi_0(\Omega_\alpha)$. Input $n$ uses the ladder over
\lean{Addr (ladderTree n)}, with the W-ranked lift hierarchy seeded with

\section{Contender 27: universes of level codes}

In Contender~26 the levels are indexed by countable codes, so iterating
$\Lambda\mapsto\psi_0(\Omega_\Lambda)$ stalls at a countable fixed point. To
pass it, level codes must branch over \emph{uncountable} level elements. Doing
that in one step would be an inductive--recursive definition, which Lean does
not have. The stratified version is available (\lean{TreeUniverses.lean}):
\begin{lstlisting}
structure Univ : Type 1 where
  Code : Type
  Low : Code → Type                         -- everything below level c
  sample : (c : Code) → ℕ → Option (Low c)  -- elements for diagonals
  top : Code
inductive NCode (U : Univ) : Type where      -- codes of the next universe
  | zero | succ (t : NCode U) | lim (f : ℕ → NCode U)
  | supAt (c : U.Code) (h : U.Low c → NCode U)
\end{lstlisting}
The next universe's \lean{Low} is defined by structural recursion on
\lean{NCode U}, so its codes denote levels as large as the elements of the
previous universe's levels. Iterating gives the tower \lean{univ k}. Each
universe has its own collapse \lean{DN}, again by structural recursion.
Successor codes diagonalize through the predecessor. Limit codes take limits.
A code whose supremum ranges over an old \lean{Low c} diagonalizes along sample
elements of \lean{Low c}. The samples are needed because these index types have
no decidable equality, but the strength comes from the diagonal.
\lean{universeLadderTree n} collapses $\varepsilon_{\Omega_{\mathrm{top}}+1}$ in
universe $n{+}1$ and uses no axioms at all. Heuristically the nesting
$\Omega_{\Omega_{\cdots}}$ deepens with~$n$, toward the first fixed point of
$\alpha\mapsto\Omega_\alpha$, an inaccessible-like level.

Contender~28 builds universes with two operations, \emph{next} and
\emph{union}. Universe-building operators can themselves be iterated
(\lean{TreeUniverses3.lean}). For $F:\mathrm{Univ}\to\mathrm{Univ}$, let
$\Phi\,F\,U$ be the union of the finite iterates $F^k(U)$, the closure point of
$F$ above $U$. Then $\Phi(\mathrm{next})$ jumps to the next universe closed
under \emph{next}, an inaccessible-like step. $\Phi(\Phi(\mathrm{next}))$ jumps
to the next limit of those, and so on. Operators are indexed by tree ordinals by
structural recursion:
\[
\Phi_0=\mathrm{next},\qquad \Phi_{a+1}=\Phi(\Phi_a),\qquad
\Phi_{\lim f}(U)=\textstyle\bigcup_i \Phi_{f\,i}(U).
\]
Each round collapses inside the universe after $\Phi_\Lambda(\mathrm{base})$,
where $\Lambda$ is the previous round's tree. \lean{operatorTree n} runs $n{+}1$
rounds from Contender~28's tree and uses no axioms. Input $n$ uses the ladder over
its addresses, with the W-ranked lift hierarchy seeded with Contender~28's
family. Proved: \lean{contender\_28\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_29},
\lean{family28\_lt\_F29} and \lean{otvalue\_lt\_F29}. The definitions check in
2.4\,s and the proofs in 2.3\,s.

\section{Contender 30: closure functionals of all finite types}

Contender~29 closes universe operators under iteration. The closure operation
itself can be lifted to every finite type over universes

A_0=\mathrm{Univ},\qquad A_{k+1}=A_k\to A_k,\qquad
\mathrm{closure}_k\,f\,a=\textstyle\bigcup_m f^m(a)\quad(f\in A_{k+1}),
\]
with unions taken pointwise at every type. $\mathrm{closure}_0$ is Contender~29's

so on. At input $n$ the chain $\mathrm{closure}_n,\dots,\mathrm{closure}_0$ is
applied to Contender~29's operator $\Phi_\Lambda$ and then to the base universe.
The resulting universe is collapsed to a countable tree, and the ladder runs over

\section{Contender 31: closure functionals of transfinite type}

The finite types of Contender~30 extend along tree ordinals
(\lean{TreeUniverses5.lean}):
\[
AT_0=\mathrm{Univ},\qquad AT_{a+1}=AT_a\to AT_a,\qquad AT_{\lim f}=\textstyle\prod_i AT_{f\,i},
\]
with pointwise unions everywhere and closure points at successor types. A
canonical functional $\mathrm{step}_a\in AT_{a+1}$ is defined by structural
recursion. It is the base operator at $0$, closure at successors, and taken
componentwise at limits. It is evaluated down to a universe along the canonical
chain of lower elements, with unions at limit types. The type level at input
$n$ is Contender~30's tree, so the height of the type hierarchy is itself a
collapsed tree. No axioms are used. Proved:

\paragraph{Where this leaves the search.} Contenders 25--31 all follow one
pattern: a countable tree ordinal is defined by structural recursion, and the
ladder runs over its addresses, with well-foundedness for free. Each step
nests the previous construction inside a stronger recursion. The true sizes are
heuristic; only the comparisons are proved. A qualitatively larger jump needs a
stronger object language, for example dependent types with large elimination,
which would require a new normalization proof.

\section{Contender 32: a programming language for tree ordinals}

Contenders 25--31 are each \emph{one} combination of tree-ordinal operations.
The natural meta-step is to make the combinations programs
(\lean{TreeLanguage.lean}). The language is Gödel's System~T with a second base
type of countable tree ordinals:
\begin{itemize}
\item types \lean{nat}, \lean{tree}, and arrows, each denoting a Lean type;
\item numerals with a recursor; \lean{tzero}, \lean{tsucc}, \lean{tlim}; a tree
  recursor that iterates along any tree at any type; and a primitive
  \lean{boost : tree => tree}, which is Contender~31's construction as a
  function of its index tree.
\end{itemize}
Unlike the impredicative ladder language, this language needs no normalization
proof. It is simply typed and predicative, so intrinsically typed terms have a
direct \emph{semantic} interpreter. Types denote Lean types, and \lean{Tm.eval}
maps terms to Lean values by structural recursion, using no axioms. A type
checker turns raw terms into typed ones. \lean{languageTree n} is the limit of
Contender~31's tree and of all closed \lean{tree} programs of size $\le n{+}3$,
so it contains transfinite and higher-type iterations of \lean{boost}: Veblen-
and Bachmann-style hierarchies over it, written as programs. The enumeration
is hopeless to run beyond tiny sizes (3.5 million raw terms at size~3), but it
is never run. The smoke test stays inside the first branch.

Proved: \lean{contender\_31\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_32}, \lean{family31\_lt\_F32}
and \lean{lgvalue\_lt\_F32}. The definitions check in 2.7\,s and the proofs in
2.2\,s.

\section{Contender 35: impredicative trees}

All the trees of Contenders~25--34 come from \emph{predicative} constructions:
collapsing functions, universes of codes, System~T over trees, and stackings of
that language. Contender~35 lets an impredicative language build the tree.

\paragraph{System FT.} \lean{SystemFT} is System~F with a base type
\lean{tree} and three inert constants
\[
\mathsf{con}_0:\mathsf{tree},\qquad
\mathsf{con}_1:\mathsf{tree}\to\mathsf{tree},\qquad
\mathsf{con}_2:(\mathsf{nat}\to\mathsf{tree})\to\mathsf{tree}.
\]
There is no eliminator for \lean{tree}. Programs compute with Church-encoded
data and then build a tree with the constructors. A polymorphic Church tree of
type $\forall X.\,X\to(X\to X)\to((\mathsf{nat}\to X)\to X)\to X$ can be
instantiated at $X=\mathsf{tree}$. So every tree that System~F can describe
becomes a real tree. The ordinals of such trees go beyond everything that
predicative constructions reach.

\paragraph{Well-foundedness by candidates.} The tree type is interpreted by
an inductive candidate $\mathrm{WT}$: a term is in $\mathrm{WT}$ when it is
strongly normalizing, all its reducts are in $\mathrm{WT}$, and
$\mathsf{con}_1\,b$ has $b\in\mathrm{WT}$ while $\mathsf{con}_2\,g$ has
$g\,\bar k\in\mathrm{WT}$ for every numeral $\bar k$. Condition CR3 of
reducibility candidates is stated for \emph{neutral} terms. Constructor-headed
terms must not count as neutral, otherwise CR3 would force
$\mathsf{con}_1\,b\in\mathrm{WT}$ without $b\in\mathrm{WT}$. With that change
the adequacy proof of System~F goes through, and
\lean{wt\_of\_typed} shows that every program of type \lean{tree} is in
$\mathrm{WT}$. Because $\mathrm{WT}$ is inductive, the child relation (reduce,
then take a constructor argument) is accessible. \lean{toTree} reads the tree off
the normal form by well-founded recursion on it. Like the normalizer it is
compiled code, and it uses only \lean{propext} and \lean{Quot.sound}.

\paragraph{The contender.} At input $n$, \lean{ftTree n} is the limit of
Contender~34's tree and the trees of all System~FT programs of depth $\le n$,
with Contender~34's family as the oracle. The ladder runs over its addresses
and is amplified by the W-ranked lift seeded with
$\mathrm{family}_{34}$. Proved: \lean{contender\_34\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_35},
\lean{family34\_lt\_F35} and \lean{ftvalue\_lt\_F35}. The definitions check in
8.0\,s, which now includes compiling System~FT, and the proofs in 3.4\,s.

\section{Contender 36: ladders that build their own length}

Contender~35 takes trees from System~F. The ladder language over the

\section{A correction: what a ladder sees}\label{sec:truncation}

From Contender~15 on, the ladders run over larger and larger well-founded
atom orders: ordinal notations, then addresses in collapsed trees. The
informal sizes given for these contenders assumed that the ladder's strength
grows with the order type of the atoms. That assumption is wrong.

A program of enumeration depth $n$ can mention only the atoms in its
enumeration list: notations of size $\le n$, or addresses of depth $\le n$.
Every typing rule that involves atoms (\lean{Fits}, \lean{apack},
\lean{aunpack}, kind instantiation with syntactic kinds) refers only to atoms
named in the derivation, and erasure forgets all types. So the set of typable
erased programs, and with it every ladder value, depends only on the finite
order on the named atoms. For address atoms its chains have length at most
$n+1$, whatever the tree. The universes over unnamed atoms exist in the
semantics, but no program can use them.

The formal results are unaffected. Each comparison theorem is proved, and every
contender does exceed the previous one. But from Contender~15 to Contender~37 the
growth came from the oracle seeding and the W-ranked amplifiers, which nest
each contender's family inside the next. It did not come from the size of the
atom order. The trees themselves are genuinely large: they are computed by
structural recursion, System~T over trees, and System~F and ladder programs.
They were just used in a place that cannot exploit them. The informal sizes in
the status table for Contenders~15--37 describe the atom orders, not the
numbers.

\section{Contender 38: a fast-growing hierarchy along the trees}

Contender~38 uses the tree as the index of a fast-growing hierarchy instead of
as ladder atoms. For a base $g$ and a tree $t$:
\[
h^g_{0}(m)=g(m),\qquad h^g_{t+1}(m)=\bigl(h^g_t\bigr)^{m+1}(m),\qquad
h^g_{\lim f}(m)=\max_{i\le m} h^g_{f\,i}(m).

the enumeration. With the maximum, every branch $i\le m$ counts. The definition
is structural recursion on \lean{CTree}.

The contender is

The base already contains all of Contender~37's amplifiers. The index contains

\section{System FT2: an impredicative jump that reads its input}

The truncation argument applies to the jump of Contenders~36 and~37 as well. A
ladder program over $\mathrm{Addr}(t)$ never inspects $t$, so the trees it builds
are the same for every $t$. Hence $\mathrm{ladderTree}(t)$ is about
$\max(t,C)+1$ for a constant $C$, and $\mathrm{bootT}$ along an index adds the
index's length. The impredicative strength sat in the constant, not in a
function of the input.

\lean{SystemFT2} fixes this. It is System~F with the tree constructors, a
\emph{tree recursor}, and an \emph{oracle tree} $T$:
\[
\mathsf{trec}:\forall X.\ X\to(X\to X)\to((\mathsf{nat}\to X)\to X)\to\mathsf{tree}\to X,
\qquad \mathsf{tin}:\mathsf{tree}.
\]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The recursor reduces on constructor forms:
  $\mathsf{trec}\,z\,s\,l\,\mathsf{con}_0\to z$,
  $\mathsf{trec}\,z\,s\,l\,(\mathsf{con}_1 b)\to s\,(\mathsf{trec}\,z\,s\,l\,b)$, and
  $\mathsf{trec}\,z\,s\,l\,(\mathsf{con}_2 g)\to l\,(\lambda k.\,\mathsf{trec}\,z\,s\,l\,(g\,k))$.
\item The input tree is read lazily by address. $\mathsf{tsub}\,p$ unfolds
  the node of $T$ at path $p$ into constructor form, and
  $\mathsf{tsubl}\,p\,\bar k\to\mathsf{tsub}(k{::}p)$ gives the branches of a
  limit node.
\end{itemize}
Two changes make the candidate proof go through.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The tree candidate requires the children $g\,a$ of $\mathsf{con}_2\,g$
  for every $a$ in the \emph{numeral candidate}, not only for numerals. The
  limit case of the recursor maps the whole candidate.
\item Partial applications of $\mathsf{trec}$ (fewer than four arguments) and
  $\mathsf{tsubl}$ are not neutral.
\end{itemize}
\lean{trec\_mem} is proved by induction on the tree candidate nested with SN
inductions on the three step functions, and \lean{wt\_tsub} by induction on
$T$. Both use only \lean{propext} and \lean{Quot.sound}.

So $\mathrm{jumpF}(t)$, the trees of all programs of depth $\le N$ that read
$t$, contains every tree System~F builds from $t$. It is a genuine
operator, and its iterates grow.

\section{Contender 42: collapsing relative to the meta-jump}

collapsing, which iteration by a language over countable trees does not.

\section{Contender 67: a reflective tower of closure operators}

Contender~66's $\mathsf{closeWith}$ closes the \emph{base} language with a
family of closures. Contender~67 raises the closing operator itself along a tree:
\[
cw_0(H)=\text{closures of }P_{66}+[H],\qquad
cw_{a+1}(H)=\text{closures of }P_{66}+[H,\ cw_a:(\mathsf{tree}\to C)\to C],
\]
with pointwise limits. The primitive types are the same at every successor
level, so one recursion on the operator suffices. A program at level $a{+}1$ can
close level~$a$'s language with families it builds, including towers of steps
built from $cw_a$ itself. Stacking (Contender~62) is the special case of a
constant family. Every level is a good closure pair for every family
(\lean{good\_cwTower}). The tower runs to the height of Contender~66's tree to
build a tree $T$, then to height $T$ for the number closure:
$F_{67}(n)=h^{cw_T(\cdot).2(\mathrm{family}_{66})}_T(N)$ with
$N=\mathrm{family}_{66}(n)$. Proved: \lean{family66\_lt\_F67} and
\lean{contender\_66\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_67}. The definitions check in
1.8\,s and the proofs in 2.5\,s.

\section{Contender 68: programs over the reflective tower's step}

Contender~67's tower of closure operators has a uniform step:
$\mathrm{cwStep}(X)(H)$ is the closure of Contender~66's language plus $H$ and the
operator $X$, and $cw_{a+1}=\mathrm{cwStep}(cw_a)$. Contender~68 makes the step
and the tower values of the language:
\[
\mathsf{cwStep}:cwTy\to cwTy,\qquad \mathsf{cwAt}:\mathsf{tree}\to cwTy,\qquad
cwTy=(\mathsf{tree}\to C)\to C .
\]
Programs iterate $\mathsf{cwStep}$ from any operator they build, not only from
$cw_0$, along trees they build. They also diagonalize, and feed one tower into
another. These primitives join Contender~66's. The tree joins Contender~67's tree
with the meta-jump's closure under this language, and the language's number
closure is the hierarchy's base:
$F_{68}(n)=h^{\mathrm{num2Lift}(\mathrm{family}_{67})}_T(N)$ with
$N=\mathrm{family}_{67}(n)$. Proved: \lean{family67\_lt\_F68} and
\lean{contender\_67\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_68}. The definitions check in
1.9\,s and the proofs in 3.5\,s.

\section{Contender 69: the reflective tower over the step language}

Contender~67 raised the closure operator of Contender~66's language along a
tree. Contender~68's language is stronger, because it contains that tower's step
and the tower itself as values. \lean{cwTowerP} is Contender~67's construction
for any base language $(P,\sigma)$:
\[
cw^P_0(H)=\text{closures of }P+[H],\qquad
cw^P_{a+1}(H)=\text{closures of }P+[H,\ cw^P_a],

with pointwise limits. Contender~69 runs it over Contender~68's language, along

$F_{69}(n)=h^{cw^{P_{68}}_T(\cdot).2(\mathrm{family}_{68})}_T(N)$ with

\section{Contender 70: the reflection cycle repeated}

Contenders~67 and~68 form a cycle. First a language's closure operator is
raised along a tree (\lean{cwTowerP}). Then that tower's step and the tower
itself become values, which gives a stronger language. Contender~69 started the
cycle again. Contender~70 repeats it $N$ times:
\[
P^R_0=P_{66},\qquad
P^R_{k+1}=\mathsf{cwStep}_{k}::\mathsf{cwAt}_{k}::P^R_k,
\]
where $\mathsf{cwStep}_k$ and $\mathsf{cwAt}_k$ are the step and the tower of the
reflective tower over the level-$k$ language. The first cycle reproduces
Contender~68's language. The reflective tower over the top language runs along
Contender~69's tree to build a tree $T$, then along $T$ for the number closure:
$F_{70}(n)=h^{cw^{P^R_N}_T(\cdot).2(\mathrm{family}_{69})}_T(N)$ with
$N=\mathrm{family}_{69}(n)$. Proved: \lean{good\_cycleTower},
\lean{family69\_lt\_F70} and \lean{contender\_69\_lt\_\allowbreak contender\_70}.
The definitions check in 1.6\,s and the proofs in 4.9\,s.

\section{An independent tower}\label{sec:independent}

From Contender~7 on, almost every contender takes its predecessor's family as
an oracle or seed. This makes each comparison short (the new family dominates
its own seed), but it also makes the tower cumulative: the size of
Contender~70 is the size of a long composition, and the article cannot say
which ideas carry it. The \emph{independent tower} (\lean{BusyLean/Independent})
is built under one extra rule: \textbf{no rung uses a smaller rung in its
definition}. Each rung is a single idea, and its comparison with the rung
below is a simulation: the larger construction runs the smaller one. When a
construction would naturally be built on a smaller rung, the smaller rung is
dropped. The rule now governs the whole contest: the cumulative tower of
Contenders 5--70 is closed and kept as a record. The independent tower has
four rungs.

\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Rung & idea & definition\\
\midrule

2 & busy beaver of System~T & largest numeral of depth 42 (Contender~5 of the Coq contest)\\
3 & ordinal notations, Buchholz's $\psi_\nu$ & $H_\theta(H_\theta(42))$, $\theta=\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_{42}+1})$\\
4 & impredicative universes building their own length & \lean{F4 (F4 42)} below\\
\bottomrule

\paragraph{Rungs 1 and 2.} The two Coq contenders are already independent of
each other. The Coq proof of \lean{contender\_4\_lt\_contender\_5} writes
Ackermann's function as a System~T term; it is ported to Lean as
\lean{contender\_1\_lt\_contender\_2}. The only subtlety is to keep Lean's
kernel from evaluating \lean{ack 5 42 10002}: every equation is stated for
symbolic arguments and instantiated afterwards.

\paragraph{Rung 3.} Ordinals are Brouwer trees (\lean{zero}, \lean{succ},
\lean{lim f}); addition, multiplication by a number and $\omega^{\cdot}$ are
defined by recursion on trees, $\omega_{k+1}=\omega^{\omega_k}$ and
$\varepsilon_0=\lim_k\omega_k$. The hierarchy is Hardy's, with one robust
choice: at a limit, $H_{\lim f}(y)=\max_{i\le y}H_{f(i)}(y)$. This makes every
$H_a$ monotone for every tree, with no condition on the fundamental sequences.
Above the Brouwer trees sit trees of every finite \emph{level}: a tree of
level $m$ has countable limits and limits $\mathtt{sup}\,\mu\,f$ of cofinality
$\Omega_{\mu+1}$, $\mu<m$, whose branches are trees of level $m-1$ (only the
lifts of trees of level $\mu$ matter). Lifting a tree one level up and
projecting it back are a single structural map. Buchholz's functions $\psi_\nu$
collapse a tree of level $n+1$ to trees of level $n$, for all $\nu\le n$ at
once, by his fundamental sequences:
\[
\psi_\nu(0)=\Omega_\nu,\quad
\psi_\nu(\alpha+1)=\lim_k\psi_\nu(\alpha)\cdot k,\quad
\psi_\nu(\lim f)=\lim_k\psi_\nu(f\,k),
\]
\[
\psi_\nu(\mathtt{sup}\,\mu\,f)=
\begin{cases}
\mathtt{sup}_\xi\,\psi_\nu(f\,\xi) & \mu<\nu,\\
\lim_k\psi_\nu(f\,g_k),\ \ g_0=\Omega_\mu,\ g_{k+1}=\psi_\mu(f\,g_k) & \mu\ge\nu.
\end{cases}
\]
The rung is $H_\theta(H_\theta(42))$ for $\theta=\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_{42}+1})$,
where $\varepsilon_{\Omega_{42}+1}=\lim(\Omega_1,\Omega_{42}^{\Omega_1},\dots)$.
Only finitely many levels can appear: rung~4 must run rung~3 as a program, and
a program mentions finitely many tree types. Buchholz's ordinal
$\psi_0(\varepsilon_{\Omega_\omega+1})$ is the limit of this family. The two
files import nothing else.

\paragraph{Rung 2 $<$ rung 3: an ordinal analysis of System~T.} An upper
bound for \emph{all} System~T values cannot come from simulation. It needs
proof theory. The proof follows Tait's infinitary terms.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Infinitary terms.} Intrinsically typed $\lambda$-terms over
  Contender~5's types, with \lean{zero}, \lean{succ} and an $\omega$-branching
  \lean{case n br} of type \lean{nat}. Natural recursion compiles to
  $\lambda a\,f\,n.\ \mathtt{case}\ n\ (a, f\,0\,a, f\,1\,(f\,0\,a),\dots)$,
  $\eta$-expanded so that the case sits at type \lean{nat}. Every raw term
  (casts included) compiles with the same value (\lean{value\_progOf}).
\item \textbf{Heights and ranks.} A term's height is its tree height, a
  Brouwer tree. A redex's level is the level of the $\lambda$'s type. Translated
  depth-42 terms have rank $<47$ and height $\le\omega^3+94$.
\item \textbf{Cut elimination.} One round contracts the redexes of the top
  level, innermost first. It creates only redexes of smaller level, and it
  raises the height to at most $\omega^{\text{height}}$ (\lean{ht\_cutElim}).
  Forty-seven rounds give a redex-free term of the same value.
\item \textbf{Bounding.} A closed redex-free term of type \lean{nat} is built
  from \lean{zero}, \lean{succ} and \lean{case}, and its value is at most
  $H_{\omega^{\text{height}}}(y)$ for $y\ge2$ (\lean{value\_le\_hardy}). Choosing
  branch $k$ is paid for by the limit at index $k$, which $H$ reaches at
  arguments $\ge k$.
\item \textbf{Comparing trees.} Trees are compared by a structural order
  $a\sqsubseteq_n b$ in which every step into one element of a fundamental
  sequence uses an index $\le n$. It is sound for $H$ at arguments $\ge n$,
  and $+$ and $\omega^{\cdot}$ preserve it. With $H_{a+b}=H_a\circ H_b$ for
  all trees, this gives
  $\mathtt{eval}\,w\le H_{\omega_{49}}(y)\le H_{\varepsilon_0}(y)$ for $y\ge200$
  (\lean{eval\_le\_hardy\_eps}).
\item \textbf{$\varepsilon_0$ inside $\theta$.} Branch~0 of $\theta$ is
  $\psi_0(\Omega_1)$, the limit of $1,\omega,\omega^\omega,\dots$: on trees
  without $\mathtt{sup}$s, $\psi_0$ is $\omega^{\cdot}$ and the level maps are
  the identity, so its Brouwer tree is exactly $\varepsilon_0$
  (\lean{toB\_branch0}). So $H_{\varepsilon_0}(y)\le H_\theta(y)$
  (\lean{Feps\_le\_F3}).
\end{enumerate}
No step evaluates a number. The main engineering hazard is Lean's own
reduction: a stray \lean{rfl}, a closing \lean{rw}, or a linter can try to
compute $H_{\varepsilon_0}(\dots)$, so the proofs generalize large terms and
keep \lean{elimAll 47} irreducible.

\paragraph{Rung 4.} The values come from the ladder of universes of
Contender~14, used as a library; its oracle constant is pointed only at the
construction itself. The lengths come from the same ladder with
\emph{transfinitely} many universes, indexed by the addresses of a tree, as
in Contender~36: a program of the Church tree type denotes a well-founded
tree.
\begin{lstlisting}
def ladderMax (g : ℕ → ℕ) (n : ℕ) : ℕ :=
  n + 1 + listMax (fvalue n g n) (ladderUpTo n)
def reflAlong : CTree → ℕ → ℕ
  | .zero => Nat.succ
  | .succ τ => ladderMax (reflAlong τ)
  | .lim g => fun n => listMax (fun i => reflAlong (g i) n) (natsUpTo (n + 1))
  | _ => Nat.succ
def grow (f : ℕ → ℕ) (n : ℕ) (t : CTree) : CTree :=
  .lim fun i => if i = 0 then t else
    ((tctmsUpTo (addrUpTo t n) n).map (progTree f t n)).getD (i - 1) .zero
def selfTree (n : ℕ) : ℕ → CTree
  | 0 => grow Nat.succ n .zero
  | k + 1 => growAlong (reflAlong (selfTree n k)) n (selfTree n k) (selfTree n k)
def F4 (n : ℕ) : ℕ := reflAlong (.succ (selfTree n n)) n
def contender_4 : ℕ := F4 (F4 42)
\end{lstlisting}
\lean{reflAlong} iterates ladder reflection along a tree. \lean{grow} joins a
tree $t$ with the trees of all programs of depth $\le n$ over the addresses of
$t$, so the ladder over $t$ produces a longer tree; \lean{growAlong} iterates
growth along an index tree. Nothing seeds the trees: the first one grows the
empty tree, and each later one grows the previous tree along itself, with
reflection along the previous tree as the oracle of the growing programs.
This gathers the ideas of Contenders 36--53 (ladders that build their own
length, reflection along ladder-built trees) into one construction. The
enumerations are redefined in the rung's own module; the audit
(\lean{test/IndependentAudit.lean}) lists the modules of the definition's
closure (the ladder libraries, the tree addresses and System~F's core) and
rejects any contender module.

\paragraph{Rung 3 $<$ rung 4: Church-encoded trees.} A small language
\lean{TT} (System~T with Brouwer trees, trees of every level and their folds)
writes the whole of rung~3 as a program of value $H_\theta(H_\theta(42))$.
Brouwer trees compile to their iterators $\forall X.\,X\to(X\to X)\to((\mathtt{nat}\to
X)\to X)\to X$, and trees of level $m$ to $\forall X.\,X\to(X\to X)\to((\mathtt{nat}\to
X)\to X)\to(\mathtt{nat}\to(D_m\to X)\to X)\to X$, where $D_m$ is the type of
level $m-1$; the $\Omega$-branching is impredicative quantification over the
type of the previous level. The logical relations are defined by recursion
on the level and the \emph{tree value}, which avoids quantifying over all
result types; the typing proofs are uniform in the level. The program has
depth 472, type checks in the ladder, and reduces to the right numeral
under every oracle. Confluence (below) lets the normalizer find it. Since
\lean{F4 n} unfolds to \lean{ladderMax (reflAlong (selfTree n n)) n}, the outer
\lean{F4} sees the compiled program.

\paragraph{Also: System~T directly into the ladder.} The first version of the
tower compared rungs 2 and 4 directly (\lean{contender\_2\_lt\_contender\_4}).
The winner of rung~2 is an arbitrary raw term of depth $\le 42$, possibly
ill-typed, and Contender~5 interprets such terms with casts and default values.
That proof therefore compiles \emph{every} raw term.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Static types.} The type tag of a term's value depends only on
  the term and the types of its environment (\lean{interp\_fst}). The
  translation uses it to resolve Contender~5's quirks statically: a cast
  between different types becomes the default value, an application of a
  number becomes $\overline{0}$.
\item \textbf{Simulation.} Natural recursion becomes iteration over
  polymorphic pairs. A logical relation (at \lean{nat}: ``reduces to the
  Church numeral'') and its fundamental lemma \lean{rel\_utr} show that a
  closed term of type \lean{nat} reduces to the numeral of its value, under any
  oracle.
\item \textbf{Confluence.} The normalizer follows normal order, while the
  simulation uses arbitrary $\beta$-steps. Takahashi's complete development proves
  Church--Rosser for $\beta$ plus the oracle rule (\lean{nf\_eq\_of\_steps}). The
  oracle fires only on literal numerals, which are normal, so it does not
  interfere.
\item \textbf{Typing.} The Church-style translation lies in the System~F
  fragment of the ladder language (no kinds but $\star$, no atoms). The
  ladder's type checker accepts it at every fuel (\lean{infer\_trC}).
\item \textbf{Size.} A depth-42 term compiles to depth at most $186$
  (\lean{cdepth\_trC}). The compiled program
  $\mathtt{natRec}\,\mathtt{succ}\,(\lambda\_\,g\,x.\,g\,(g\,x))\,10\,0 = 2^{10}$
  fits in depth 42, so \lean{F4 42} $\ge 1067$, and the outer \lean{F4} sees
  the compiled winner.
\end{enumerate}
The definitions of all four rungs check in about 3~s and the three comparisons,
the ordinal analysis included, in about 40~s.

\paragraph{What was left out, and why.} System~F, F$_\omega$, kind
polymorphism and the dynamic-kind universe are fragments of the ladder
language, and the W-language and the tree languages extend System~T, so each
would be incorporated by a larger rung. Larger ordinal notations (Contenders
16--31) are the same approach as rung~3, which now uses Buchholz's
functions with 42 uncountable levels. Towers, closure operators and reflective
towers (Contenders 6, 9--12, 15, 23 and 54--70) are amplifiers of a base
language; two of them, ladders that build their own length and reflection
along trees (Contenders 36--53), are built into rung~4.

\section{Roadmap}

The construction now has three layers. Their weak points mark where further
growth must come from.

\paragraph{Languages that read trees.} The impredicative languages (System~FT2
and the ladder language FLT2) recurse over an input tree at every type. They
build trees from it (the jumps of Contenders~40--41) and compute numbers from
it (the reflections of Contenders~49--50). Their strength is fixed by the
language: System~F, or the ladder with a universe for each named atom. The
next step up is a stronger reading language. One option is type-level
recursion over the input tree, so that universes are indexed by the tree's
nodes and not just by named atoms. This is large elimination, the route to a
CIC-like system, and it needs a new strong normalization proof.

\paragraph{Using the atom order.} Section~\ref{sec:truncation} showed that a
ladder sees only the atoms it names. Quantification over atoms, such as a type
former $\forall b<a.\,A$ interpreted as an intersection over all atoms below $a$,
would let programs act uniformly across the transfinite order. Whether that
adds strength without recursion over atoms is open. Atom recursion would.

\paragraph{Operators on trees.} Contenders~42--48 stack operators on trees:
collapsing relative to a jump, collapse as a first-class closure, the
construction as its own base, and a Veblen-style hierarchy of iteration
levels. The natural continuation is collapsing at this level, with iteration
levels indexed by higher tree classes and then collapsed, as Buchholz's
functions collapse the levels $\Omega_\nu$. A complementary step is a
metalanguage whose primitives include the iteration operators themselves, so
that programs can iterate them at higher types.

\paragraph{Reading the final tree.} Contenders~49 and~50 turn the final tree
into a number with programs that recurse over it. Any stronger reading
language from the first paragraph improves this step directly.

\paragraph{Ladder lengths.} The ordinal-notation ladders of Contenders~15--24
remain correct and well-founded, but by Section~\ref{sec:truncation} their
length does not reach the numbers. Their well-foundedness proofs are reusable
wherever a genuine transfinite recursion is needed.
