Contender 20: Second-Order Diagonal Hyperreflection
in BusyLean
A constructive type-level jump beyond the large-Veblen contender
Research proposal for the BusyLean project
13 October 2026

Abstract
BusyLean is a Lean 4 version of the “name the biggest number” contest: every entry is a closed
computable natural number and every victory claim is a formal strict inequality. At the audited
baseline, Contender 19 combines a large-Veblen-length ladder of strongly normalizing object
languages with a natural-rank hierarchy of transformer lifts. This article proposes Contender 20,
whose main novelty is not a longer ordinal notation and not one more numerical iteration, but a
jump in the type of the amplifier.
We introduce a hyperlift, an endomorphism of transformer lifts. We define countable splicing of
lifts, W-tree traversal by an arbitrary hyperlift, an autonomous second-order lift, and a diagonal
hierarchy of hyperlifts. The proofs isolate a reusable invariant: a good lift both preserves
progressive transformers and strictly exceeds its input transformer. The second-order hierarchy
preserves good lifts and strictly exceeds its base lift, without any monotonicity assumption on
oracle reflection.
At each input n, the new function first embeds a complete first-order hyperreflection layer of
rank family19 (n) + 1 and then applies a second-order diagonal hierarchy of the same rank. The
formal comparison is factored through the explicit chain
family19 (n) < firstOrder20 (n) < F20 (n).
Consequently the proposed closed number
contender20 = F20 (F20 (42))
strictly exceeds Contender 19. All new definitions are structural and computable. The package
includes Lean source, comparison theorems, a repository patch, a smoke test, a static constructivity audit, and this compiled article. The LaTeX and static audits were executed; a Lean 4.34.0
kernel build remains a required pre-merge step because that toolchain was unavailable in the
package generation environment.

1

Problem, baseline, and result

1.1

The contest rule

The governing principle is stronger than merely writing an expression that is obviously enormous.
A valid contender must satisfy three requirements.
1. It is a closed Lean term of type Nat.
2. It is computable in principle: no appeal to a Busy Beaver, an unbounded search whose termination
is postulated, or classical choice.
3. Its superiority over the incumbent is certified by a theorem accepted by Lean’s kernel.
The values in question must never be evaluated. Their magnitude is established extensionally,
through generic strict-growth lemmas whose proof terms are small. That distinction—huge definitions
but small comparison proofs—is central to BusyLean’s engineering.

The repository pins Lean v4.34.0. Its README names Contender 19 as the current entry. In

def F19 (n : Nat) : Nat :=

The object-language component, lTransformer, reflects programs in a ladder whose atoms

endomorphisms of transformers. The repository proves
theorem contender_18_lt_contender_19 :
contender_18 < contender_19

and, more strongly, pointwise domination of the entire predecessor family.

Why a type-level jump is the right next move

Several simpler definitions would win the literal comparison but would not be serious successors.

2.1

Trivial and near-trivial victories

The definitions

contender19 +1,

F19 (F19 (F19 (42))),

or

k
F19
(42)

for some fixed k are computable and easy to compare. They add no new mathematical mechanism.
BusyLean’s accepted sequence has consistently sought a new language, a new reflection principle,
a longer well-founded universe ladder, or a new operator-level closure. Merely inserting another
numeral or another fixed composition would be contrary to that trajectory.

2.2

A longer ordinal ladder

A natural alternative is to replace the large-Veblen atom order by a stronger notation system—for
example, a collapsing-function system associated with a Bachmann–Howard or stronger ordinal.
That direction remains important, but it has a high formal cost. One must define normal forms,
a decidable comparison, a finite enumerator, and a direct well-foundedness proof strong enough
for FL.wfUniv. The current large-Veblen development is already a substantial proof-engineering
achievement. A rushed collapsing system would create much more proof risk than the present
proposal.
Moreover, an ordinal extension changes the length of the object-language ladder while leaving the
amplifier type unchanged. The present construction is orthogonal: it closes over the entire amplifier
used by Contender 19. Eventually the two ideas should be combined.

2.3

Reflection over Lean itself

One can imagine enumerating kernel-checked Lean terms and taking a bounded maximum. Such
a construction could be exceptionally strong, but a trustworthy version requires a reflected Lean
kernel, a certified evaluator, a finite coding discipline, and careful avoidance of self-reference. That
is a separate research program, not an incremental repository patch.

2.4

The chosen principle

Contender 19’s diagonalLift already treats a transformer lift as an object that can be iterated and
diagonally reused. The most canonical next step is therefore to treat that whole lift as data for a
higher-order operator. The resulting code is compact because the generic proof architecture can be
raised one level almost verbatim. The increase is nevertheless structural: the carrier changes from
Transformer → Transformer
to

The inherited algebra

This section restates the existing abstractions needed by the proposal. The exact Lean definitions
are in MetaReflection.lean, HyperReflection.lean, and their facts modules.

3.1

Families and transformers

A family is a countable two-argument array of natural numbers,
Family = N → N → N.
A transformer maps one family to another,
Transformer = Family → Family.
The first argument of a family is a visible coordinate; the second is the main numerical input.
Definition 3.1 (Progressive transformer). A transformer T is progressive when every visible value
of its input family is strictly below every output coordinate at the same numerical input:
Progressive(T ) :⇐⇒ ∀s, i, o, n, i ≤ n =⇒ s(i, n) < T (s)(o, n).
This property is stronger than the one inequality needed for a contender, and that strength is
what makes it compositional.

3.2

Transformer lifts

A transformer lift is an endomorphism of transformers,
TransformerLift = Transformer → Transformer.
Two predicates govern a lift L.
PreservesProgressive(L) :⇐⇒ ∀T, Progressive(T ) =⇒ Progressive(L(T )),
StrictlyInflationary(L) :⇐⇒ ∀T, Progressive(T ) =⇒
∀s, i, o, n, i ≤ n =⇒ T (s)(i, n) < L(T )(s)(o, n).
The strict predicate is intentionally pointwise and coordinate-flexible. It does not assert that L
is monotone as a function of T or of the oracle family. Oracle programs may branch on numerical
answers, so such monotonicity would be false in general.

3.3

Tree stages and first-order diagonalization

Given a local lift L and a base transformer T , the existing stageLift L T a traverses a countably
branching well-founded tree a. At a leaf it applies L to T ; at a node it recursively constructs one
child transformer per coordinate, splices the children, and applies L to the splice.
For a fixed schedule a, diagonalLift a k is a natural-rank hierarchy:
D0 (T ) = liftTransformer(T ),
Dk+1 (T ) = stageLift(Dk , Dk (T ), a).
The same lower rank Dk appears twice at a successor: as the local lift and as the first amplification
of the base. This is the first-order diagonal pattern that the proposal raises one type level.

Second-order definitions

4.1

Hyperlifts

Definition 4.1 (Hyperlift). A hyperlift is an endomorphism of transformer lifts:
HyperLift = TransformerLift → TransformerLift.
In Lean:
abbrev HyperLift := TransformerLift → TransformerLift

The name is descriptive rather than foundational: no claim is made that this is a canonical
hierarchy in proof theory. It is simply the next endomorphism level required by the existing design.

4.2

Splicing lifts

Existing spliceTransformers lets output coordinate i choose transformer i. The second-order
analogue is pointwise in the supplied base transformer:
def spliceLifts (ops : Nat → TransformerLift) : TransformerLift :=
fun base => spliceTransformers (fun i => ops i base)

Thus, after a base transformer T is supplied,
spliceLifts(L0 , L1 , . . .)(T )
is the splice of L0 (T ), L1 (T ), . . .. Coordinate i selects Li (T ) and uses that transformer’s canonical
output coordinate zero.

4.3

Tree traversal by a hyperlift

def stageHyperLift (hyper : HyperLift) (base : TransformerLift) :
WTree Nat → TransformerLift
| .leaf => hyper base
| .node f =>
hyper (spliceLifts (fun i => stageHyperLift hyper base (f i)))

This is exactly the same W-tree pattern one type level higher. Structural recursion on WTree
Nat supplies computability. No search, quotient choice, or semantic oracle is introduced.

4.4

The autonomous second-order lift

A naive type-level analogue of autonomous lifting would be L 7→ (T 7→ stageLift(L, T, index)). For
the comparison proof we want the result to strictly exceed L itself, not merely the base transformer
T . We therefore diagonally seed the stage with L(T ):
def liftLift (lift : TransformerLift) : TransformerLift :=
fun base => stageLift lift (lift base) index

For a good L, two strict steps are now visible:
T < L(T ) < stageLift(L, L(T ), index).
The first is strict inflationarity of L; the second is the existing base_lt_stageLift theorem.
7

4.5

Second-order diagonal ranks

def diagonalHyperLift (schedule : WTree Nat) : Nat → HyperLift
| 0, base => liftLift base
| k + 1, base =>
stageHyperLift (diagonalHyperLift schedule k)
(diagonalHyperLift schedule k base) schedule

Writing Hk for diagonalHyperLift schedule k,
H0 (L) = liftLift(L),
Hk+1 (L) = stageHyperLift(Hk , Hk (L), schedule).
At each successor rank the preceding hyper-rank appears extensionally as the local operator and
intensionally in the seed. The recurrence is a literal second-order analogue of diagonalLift.

5

The invariant: good lifts

5.1

Definition

Definition 5.1 (Good transformer lift). A transformer lift is good when it both preserves progressive
transformers and strictly exceeds its input transformer:
def GoodLift (lift : TransformerLift) : Prop :=
PreservesProgressive lift ∧ StrictlyInflationary lift

For a hyperlift H we use two properties.
PreservesGoodLifts(H) :⇐⇒ ∀L, GoodLift(L) =⇒ GoodLift(H(L)),
StrictlyLiftInflationary(H) :⇐⇒ ∀L, T, GoodLift(L) ∧ Progressive(T )
=⇒ ∀s, i, o, n, i ≤ n =⇒ L(T )(s)(i, n) < H(L)(T )(s)(o, n).
The second property is the exact higher-order analogue of strict inflationarity. It compares two
lifts after applying both to the same progressive base transformer.

5.2

Splice closure

Lemma 5.2 (Splicing preserves goodness). If every Li is good, then spliceLifts (fun i => L_i)
is good.
Proof architecture. For progressivity preservation, apply each Li to a progressive base transformer
and invoke the existing spliceTransformers_progressive. For strict inflationarity, output coordinate o selects Lo . Its strictness compares the base transformer’s visible coordinate i with canonical
output coordinate zero of Lo (T ). This is definitionally the selected splice value.
No comparison between different Li is needed.

8

5.3

The rank-zero hyperlift

Theorem 5.3 (liftLift_preservesGoodLifts). The autonomous second-order lift maps good
lifts to good lifts.
Proof architecture. Let L be good and T progressive. Then L(T ) is progressive. The existing
stageLift_progressive theorem shows that
stageLift(L, L(T ), index)
is progressive. For strict inflationarity, chain
T (s)(i, n) < L(T )(s)(i, n) < stageLift(L, L(T ), index)(s)(o, n).
The first inequality uses strictness of L and the second uses base_lt_stageLift with base L(T ).
Theorem 5.4 (liftLift_strictlyLiftInflationary). The autonomous second-order lift strictly
exceeds its input lift.
Proof architecture. For good L and progressive T , apply base_lt_stageLift directly to the base
transformer L(T ). The target is definitionally liftLift L T.

5.4

Tree-stage closure

Theorem 5.5 (stageHyperLift_good). If a hyperlift preserves good lifts and its base lift is good,
then every W-tree stage is good.
Proof architecture. Induct structurally on the W-tree. At a leaf, apply the preservation hypothesis.
At a node, recursively obtain a good lift for each child, splice them using the splice lemma, and
apply the preservation hypothesis to the splice.
Theorem 5.6 (base_lt_stageHyperLift). If a hyperlift preserves good lifts and strictly exceeds
its input lift, then every W-tree stage strictly exceeds its base lift.
Proof architecture. Induct on the W-tree. The leaf case is the strictness hypothesis. At a node, first
follow child zero, obtaining
L(T )(s)(i, n) < child0 (T )(s)(0, n).
The child lifts are good, so their splice is good. Apply hyperlift strictness to the splice at visible
coordinate zero. By the definition of spliceLifts, that visible coordinate is exactly child zero. The
two strict inequalities compose.
The explicit child-zero witness is important. It avoids any need to prove that the hyperlift is
monotone in its input lift.

5.5

Diagonal hierarchy

Theorem 5.7 (Preservation at every second-order rank). The declaration diagonalHyperLift_
preservesGoodLifts states that every natural rank of diagonalHyperLift preserves good lifts.
Proof architecture. Induct on the rank. Rank zero is liftLift_preservesGoodLifts. At a successor, the induction hypothesis makes Hk (L) good; then stageHyperLift_good applies with local
hyperlift Hk and base lift Hk (L).
9

Theorem 5.8 (Strict growth at every second-order rank). The declaration diagonalHyperLift_
strictlyLiftInflationary states that every natural rank of diagonalHyperLift strictly exceeds
its original base lift.
Proof architecture. Again induct on the rank. Rank zero is the strict theorem for liftLift. At a
successor, first use the induction hypothesis to obtain
L(T ) < Hk (L)(T ).
The preservation theorem makes Hk (L) good. Then base_lt_stageHyperLift gives
Hk (L)(T ) < Hk+1 (L)(T ).
Compose the inequalities.
A one-step rank theorem is extracted as a separate public lemma:
theorem diagonalHyperLift_rank_lt_succ ... :
diagonalHyperLift schedule k base op seed i n <
diagonalHyperLift schedule (k + 1) base op seed o n

This theorem is not required for the final contender comparison, but it exposes the internal
strict chain across all predecessor-sized ranks.

Definition of Contender 20

6.1

The predecessor family

As in previous entries, the closed predecessor number is converted back into a pointwise family:
def family19 (n : Nat) : Nat := F19 (F19 n)

Definitionally,

family19 (42) = contender19 .

Using a family rather than the closed constant avoids hard-coding the incumbent and yields a
stronger pointwise theorem.

6.2

Schedule

def schedule20 (n : Nat) : WTree Nat :=
Autonomous.realize
(Autonomous.hyper (family19 n + 42)
(Autonomous.encode (schedule19 n)))

The new schedule is not merely schedule19 n. It starts from the encoded Contender 19 schedule
and applies a predecessor-sized number of higher Autonomous.hyper derivatives. These trees are
syntax used as schedules; no semantic order comparison or quotient-level ordinal identity is required.

10

6.3

Rank and embedded first-order layer

def rank20 (n : Nat) : Nat := family19 n + 1
def baseLift20 (n : Nat) : TransformerLift :=
diagonalLift (schedule20 n) (rank20 n)
def firstOrder20 (n : Nat) : Nat :=
baseLift20 n lTransformer (fun _ => family19) n n

Thus firstOrder20 n is already a complete first-order diagonal hyperreflection using
• the large-Veblen reflection transformer lTransformer,
• the predecessor family as its packed oracle seed,
• a predecessor-sized Autonomous.hyper closure constructed from Contender 19’s schedule, and
• a rank strictly larger than family19 n.
This definition is the formal witness separating a serious construction from a syntactic victory.

6.4

Second-order closure

def F20 (n : Nat) : Nat :=
diagonalHyperLift (schedule20 n) (rank20 n)
(baseLift20 n) lTransformer (fun _ => family19) n n
def contender_20 : Nat := F20 (F20 42)

There are two nested diagonal hierarchies of the same predecessor-controlled rank:
1. baseLift20 n is a first-order hierarchy of transformer lifts;
2. diagonalHyperLift ... (baseLift20 n) is a second-order hierarchy of hyperlifts acting on
that complete first-order hierarchy.
The outer self-application in contender_20 follows the established BusyLean convention and
provides a clean final comparison step.

7

Formal dominance chain

7.1

The base lift is good

The existing first-order theorems immediately package the embedded lift:
theorem baseLift20_good (n : Nat) : GoodLift (baseLift20 n) :=
⟨diagonalLift_preservesProgressive (schedule20 n) (rank20 n),
diagonalLift_strictlyInflationary (schedule20 n) (rank20 n)⟩

This small theorem is the bridge from the existing repository to the new second-order theory.

11

7.2

First strict step

Because a good lift preserves progressivity, applying baseLift20 n to the progressive lTransformer
yields a progressive transformer. Feed it the constant family of family19 coordinates and select
visible coordinate zero:
Theorem 7.1 (family19_lt_firstOrder20). For every n,
family19 (n) < firstOrder20 (n).
The proof is a direct application of progressivity. It does not inspect any large numerical value.

7.3

Second strict step

Apply the generic second-order strictness theorem to baseLift20 n and lTransformer:
Theorem 7.2 (firstOrder20_lt_F20). For every n,
firstOrder20 (n) < F20 (n).
This is the essential new inequality. Its left side is an entire predecessor-sized first-order hierarchy,
not merely the predecessor family.

7.4

Pointwise predecessor domination

Transitivity gives
Theorem 7.3 (family19_lt_F20). For every n,
family19 (n) < F20 (n).
The repository already proves n < F19 (n), so
n < F19 (n) < F19 (F19 (n)) = family19 (n) < F20 (n).
Hence n < F20 (n) for every n.

7.5

Closed contender comparison

At n = 42,

contender19 = family19 (42) < F20 (42).

Applying the input-growth theorem at F20 (42) yields
F20 (42) < F20 (F20 (42)) = contender20 .
Therefore:
Theorem 7.4 (contender_19_lt_contender_20).
contender19 < contender20 .
The Lean proof is only one transitivity step:
theorem contender_19_lt_contender_20 : contender_19 < contender_20 :=
Nat.lt_trans (family19_lt_F20 42) (input_lt_F20 (F20 42))

9.3

No oracle monotonicity assumption

A tempting but invalid proof strategy would be to claim that a larger oracle family always produces
a larger reflected value. Reflected programs can inspect oracle answers and branch, so output need
not be monotone. The proposal follows the existing repository discipline instead:
1. progressivity exposes a concrete seed value inside each transformed family;
2. splicing exposes a concrete selected child;
3. strictness follows a fixed child-zero path;
4. all final comparisons compose these explicit occurrences.
This is why the proofs remain valid for arbitrary progressive transformers, not only for a specially
monotone object language.

10

How large is the increase?

10.1

What is formally established

The kernel-level claim is exact and modestly stated:
contender19 < contender20 .
More structurally, the formal proof establishes the pointwise sandwich
family19 (n) < firstOrder20 (n) < F20 (n),
where the middle term is a complete first-order diagonal hierarchy with predecessor-sized rank and a
schedule syntactically generated from the predecessor schedule by a predecessor-sized hyper-closure.

10.2

Why this is more than another natural rank

Suppose, schematically, that a transformer lift L is an amplification rule on transformers. Contender 19 constructs many such L’s and diagonalizes among them, but the rank remains a naturalnumber recursion inside the type Transformer → Transformer.
Contender 20 forms operators
H : (Transformer → Transformer) → (Transformer → Transformer)
and diagonalizes among those. The outer recurrence can use an entire lower lift hierarchy as both
its local operator and its seed. In analogy with classical fast-growth constructions, this resembles
moving from iterating a fixed normal function to iterating a closure operator that itself manufactures
whole function hierarchies. The analogy is heuristic; the Lean theorem does not assign a canonical
ordinal index to these operators.

14

10.3

Why no exact ordinal calibration is claimed

The BusyLean construction combines several ingredients whose interaction is not captured by a
standard published hierarchy:
• a strongly normalizing large-Veblen universe ladder;
• dynamic oracle families;
• W-tree schedules generated from language-defined trees;
• transformer-level autonomous closure;
• first- and second-order diagonal reuse;
• ranks and schedules numerically controlled by the predecessor family.
Assigning a proof-theoretic ordinal would require a lower-bound interpretation and a matching
upper-bound analysis for this precise higher-order reflection algebra. Any simple expression such as
“fα for this named α” would currently be conjectural. The article therefore makes only the robust
structural comparison supported by the source.

10.4

A useful lower-bound reading

Even without an ordinal label, the formal intermediate witness yields a clear lower-bound narrative.
At input n, F20 (n) exceeds:
1. the complete predecessor family value family19 n;
2. one application of a predecessor-sized diagonalLift hierarchy to the large-Veblen reflection
transformer;
3. that whole first-order layer after it has been installed as the base of a predecessor-sized secondorder diagonal hierarchy.
Thus the margin is internal and compositional, not merely a consequence of the outer definition
F20 (F20 42).

11

Lean engineering considerations

11.1

Avoiding enormous reduction

The main practical danger in BusyLean proofs is asking the elaborator or simplifier to compare
terms by normalizing a contender-sized expression. The new modules avoid that pattern.
• No proof uses simp [F20], decide, or arithmetic evaluation on family19.
• The final theorem uses only previously proved strict inequalities and Nat.lt_trans.
• Definitions are unfolded only at small algebraic interfaces such as spliceLifts and one W-tree
constructor equation.
• The smoke test contains #check and #print axioms, never #eval or #reduce.
15

11.2

Expected compilation profile

The new code is about four hundred lines including documentation and smoke checks. Its proofs are
generic and recurse only over theorem parameters, not concrete gigantic ranks. The concrete numeral
rank20 n is passed as an opaque argument to already compiled recursive definitions. Accordingly,
the expected elaboration cost is governed by the size of the proof terms, not by the value of the
contender.
This expectation must still be tested on Lean 4.34.0. The repository’s timing rules make build
performance part of acceptance.

11.3

Potential elaboration sensitivities

Three local points deserve attention during the first build.
1. Lean must accept the structurally recursive theorem equations for stageHyperLift_good and
base_lt_stageHyperLift. They mirror accepted equations in HyperReflectionFacts.
2. One change statement relies on definitional reduction of spliceLifts, spliceTransformers,
and the node equation of stageHyperLift. If reducibility settings interfere, replace it with
explicit local rfl equalities.
3. Implicit inference must resolve the nested property predicates. All applications are fully staged
enough that any repair should be a local type annotation, not a mathematical change.

13

Further research

13.1

Third and higher operator levels

The next literal step is to define
UltraLift = HyperLift → HyperLift
and repeat the same construction. Hand-writing every finite level is possible but conceptually
unsatisfactory. A more valuable project is a dependent family of carriers
A0 = Transformer,

Ak+1 = Ak → Ak ,

together with recursively defined goodness, splicing, tree stages, and strict inflationarity. The
challenge is to package the varying types so that a runtime-selected finite level can still be collapsed
to a natural number.

13.2

Transfinite hyper-ranks

Current ranks are natural numbers, although each rank can contain enormous tree schedules.
One could index hyper-ranks by the already formalized large-Veblen order. A successful design
would require a well-founded recursion whose successor/limit behavior preserves the explicit-witness
strictness proofs. This may be a cleaner route to transfinite operator order than extending the
object-language atom ladder again.

13.3

Combining operator order with stronger ordinal atoms

Second-order hyperreflection is orthogonal to the choice of lTransformer. If BusyLean later
formalizes a Bachmann–Howard, Takeuti–Feferman, or stronger well-founded atom order, the new
reflection transformer can be substituted beneath baseLift20. The second-order library should
require no change.
17

13.4

Ordinal calibration

A serious calibration program should separate four questions.
1. What standard hierarchy embeds into one progressive reflection transformer over the large-Veblen
ladder?
2. What closure corresponds to liftTransformer and diagonalLift?
3. What new closure is introduced by liftLift and diagonalHyperLift?
4. Can matching upper bounds be proved by assigning ordinal diagrams to transformer and hyperlift
terms?
Without both lower and upper bounds, named-ordinal estimates should remain explicitly
heuristic.

13.5

A certified Lean-kernel reflection contender

Longer term, BusyLean could internalize a finite fragment of Lean’s kernel or a small dependent
type theory strong enough to verify the existing contender modules. A bounded maximum over
certified closed programs would subsume many hand-designed object languages. The key research
problem is preserving constructive executability while avoiding circular soundness claims.

13.6

Performance instrumentation

The repository would benefit from a standard benchmark script recording:
• clean focused build time for each comparison module;
• maximum resident memory;
• axiom reports for contender and comparison theorem;
• source scan for forbidden constructs;
• proof-term size and imported declaration count.
Such data would make future proposals easier to compare on engineering quality, not only on
mathematical ambition.

14

Conclusion

Contender 20 advances BusyLean along the most direct structural axis left open by Contender 19.
The incumbent already has a powerful object language and a first-order diagonal hierarchy of
transformer lifts. The proposal does not merely lengthen that natural rank. It promotes whole
lift hierarchies to the objects manipulated by a new hyperlift level, proves that the corresponding
second-order diagonal ranks preserve the exact strict-growth invariant needed by the contest, and
embeds a complete predecessor-sized first-order layer as an explicit lower witness.
The resulting formal chain is simple to state:
family19 (n) < firstOrder20 (n) < F20 (n),
18

and it yields

contender19 < contender20 .

All definitions are structural and intended to remain within BusyLean’s constructive rules. The
remaining task before acceptance is concrete rather than conceptual: build the overlay with
Lean 4.34.0, inspect its axiom report, and verify that focused and full builds remain inside the
repository’s timing budget.
