Contender 7: Autonomous Operator Reflection in Lean
A constructive large-number proposal for the BusyLean repository
Research artifact prepared for the BusyLean project
12 October 2026

Abstract
The BusyLean repository formalizes a Lean edition of the “name the biggest number” contest. Its
current Contender 6 applies forest reflection over a finite-type W-language along a universal W-tree
that joins every tree definable in that language. This article proposes a next entry, Contender 7, by
lifting the same construction one type level: from oracle families to transformers of oracle families.
The primitive reflection operation is packaged as a transformer; arbitrary transformers are run along
W-trees; the resulting autonomous lift is itself iterated along the universal tree. The closed Lean
definition is
contender_7 = OpStage(Ref, Index)(P5)(42, 42).
The supplied Lean proof source establishes the pointwise inequality
finalFamily n < autonomousFamily n n,
and hence
contender_6 < contender_7.
The proof uses explicit child occurrences and never assumes monotonicity of reflection in its oracle, a
property known to fail for the underlying object language. Under the repository’s informal calibration
λ ≈ ψ(Ωω ), Contender 6 has scale roughly fλ2 (42), while the operator-level autonomous closure
proposed here has the heuristic scale fλλ (42). The strict inequality is a formal Lean theorem
statement with proof source; the ordinal calibration is deliberately presented only as a heuristic.
The bundle also contains source audits, smoke tests, a clean-build script, a unified patch, and this
compiled article. Because the execution environment used to prepare the bundle lacks Lean 4.34.0,
final kernel compilation and timing remain a required integration check rather than a claimed result.

Autonomous operator reflection

Problem and repository baseline

BusyLean changes the usual large-number game in two decisive ways. A contender must denote a closed,
computable natural number, and superiority over the previous contender must be justified by a proof
checked by Lean’s kernel. The rules exclude nonconstructive or implementation-only escape hatches
such as noncomputable, partial, unsafe, opaque stand-ins, implemented_by, external primitives,
native_decide, custom axioms, and appeals to uncomputable functions such as Busy Beaver. They
also impose separate checking budgets for definitions and proofs.
The repository revision used by this artifact is
86b360648163f0249eab79539438b6a83dfe8215.
At that revision the incumbent is
def contender_6 : Nat := stage p5Family index 42

and the principal comparison theorem is
theorem contender_5_lt_contender_6 : contender_5 < contender_6

The source is organized so that the definition layer can be checked without normalizing the enormous
number. This proposal preserves that architecture: definitions live in MetaReflection.lean and
Contender7.lean; proofs live in MetaReflectionFacts.lean and Comparison7.lean.

1.1

Why a mere arithmetic wrapper is inadequate

There are trivial ways to obtain a larger number, for example contender_6 + 1 or 2contender_6 . Such
definitions are computable and easy to compare, but they violate the scientific intent of the contest’s
“no laziness” rule: they exploit the previous contender as data and add only a superficial arithmetic
shell. The new definition must instead strengthen the generating mechanism and should not mention
the constant contender_6 at all.
Contender 7 meets this stronger criterion. Its definition imports the W-language, universal tree,
and ordinary-reflection machinery behind Contender 6, but not the previous named value. It introduces
a new recursion at the operator level and proves the old family is embedded strictly below it.

2

Contender 6 in functional form

The design of Contender 7 is easiest to understand by restating the incumbent as a functional construction.

2.1

Families and ordinary reflection

Let

F =N→N→N

be the type of countable families of unary numerical functions. In Lean this is the abbreviation
abbrev Family := Nat → Nat → Nat

The first coordinate selects an oracle member; the second is the numerical input. The W-language
interpreter is evaluated in an environment whose oldest variable denotes such a family. The operation
Ref(f s, n) strictly exceeds both:

1. every visible oracle value f s(i, n) with i ≤ n, and
2. every enumerated program of depth at most n, evaluated relative to f s.
The implementation is a finite maximum plus a positive offset, so it is constructive and total.

2.2

Forest reflection

The type WTree Nat has a leaf constructor and countably branching nodes:
a ::= leaf | node(f ),

f : N → WTree(N).

Ordinary forest reflection is
stage(s, leaf)(n) = Ref(s, n),
stage(s, node(f ))(n) = Ref (i, m) 7→ stage(s, f (i))(m), n .


At a node, the child stages are retained as a family rather than collapsed pointwise. A program may
therefore compute which child to call. This is crucial: a simple pointwise maximum would erase the
dynamic indexing power of the oracle language.

2.3

The universal index

For a program t, defTree t interprets it and casts the result to a WTree Nat, using a leaf on type
mismatch. The tree treesOfDepth k joins the trees denoted by every program enumerated at depth at
most k. Finally,
def index : WTree Nat := .node treesOfDepth

joins those finite-depth joins for every k. Thus index does not encode a hand-written ordinal notation
system; it ranges over every W-tree that the object language itself defines.
Writing Index for this tree and s = P5 for the P5 seed family, Contender 6 is
C6 = stage(s, Index)(42).
Equivalently, after packaging reflection as a family transformer, it will be one autonomous lift of the
primitive transformer. This reformulation reveals the next natural closure operation.

From functions to operators

3.1

Transformers

Definition 3.1 (Transformer). A transformer is a function from oracle families to oracle families:
T = F → F.
In Lean:
abbrev Transformer := Family → Family

The ordinary reflection step is packaged as the transformer
R(f s)(o, n) = Ref(f s, n),
where the output coordinate o is intentionally ignored.

def reflectionTransformer : Transformer :=
fun fs _ n => reflect fs n

The redundant coordinate is not cosmetic. Once transformers are combined, that coordinate becomes
the routing channel through which a higher-level operator selects a child transformer.

3.2

Running a transformer along a tree

Definition 3.2 (Transformer run). For T ∈ T , seed s ∈ F, and W-tree a, define Run(T, s, a) ∈ F
recursively by
Run(T, s, leaf) = T (s),
Run(T, s, node(f )) = T (i, n) 7→ Run(T, s, f (i))(i, n) .


The aligned coordinate i in the recursive clause is important. Coordinate i of the family supplied to
T contains child i, evaluated at child output coordinate i. Thus a transformer can inspect or route
through the entire countable forest.
The Lean definition is structural:
def runTransformer (op : Transformer) (seed : Family) :
WTree Nat → Family
| .leaf => op seed
| .node f =>
op (fun i n => runTransformer op seed (f i) i n)

No general recursion, termination oracle, or postulated well-founded relation is required; recursion is
directly on the inductive W-tree.

3.3

Autonomous lifting

Definition 3.3 (Autonomous lift). The lift of a transformer runs it along the universal index:
Lift(T )(s) = Run(T, s, Index).
def liftTransformer (op : Transformer) : Transformer :=
fun seed => runTransformer op seed index

For the primitive reflection transformer, this recovers the incumbent forest reflection exactly.
Theorem 3.4 (Compatibility with Contender 6). For every seed family s, output coordinate o, and
input n,
Lift(R)(s)(o, n) = stage(s, Index)(n).
The supplied Lean theorem is
theorem liftReflection_eq_stage (seed : Family) (o n : Nat) :
liftTransformer reflectionTransformer seed o n =
stage seed index n

It follows by structural induction on the W-tree, not by extensional or ordinal reasoning.

A single application of Lift turns ordinary reflection into the mechanism behind Contender 6. Contender 7
makes Lift itself the operation that is iterated.

Splicing child transformers

Given a countable family (Ti )i∈N of transformers, define their splice by
splice(T0 , T1 , . . .)(s)(i, n) = Ti (s)(0, n).
Output coordinate i chooses the i-th transformer. Coordinate zero is the canonical observation point
inside the selected child.
def spliceTransformers (ops : Nat → Transformer) : Transformer :=
fun seed i n => ops i seed 0 n

4.2

The outer recursion

Definition 4.1 (Operator stage). For a base transformer B, define a tree-indexed transformer
OpStage(B, a) by
OpStage(B, leaf) = Lift(B),
OpStage(B, node(f )) = Lift splice(i 7→ OpStage(B, f (i))) .


In Lean:
def operatorStage (base : Transformer) : WTree Nat → Transformer
| .leaf => liftTransformer base
| .node f =>
liftTransformer
(spliceTransformers (fun i => operatorStage base (f i)))

This is the central new definition. There are now two autonomous W-tree layers:
1. the inner universal index inside every application of Lift, and
2. the outer tree controlling how those lifted transformers are spliced and lifted again.
Choosing the universal index for the outer tree closes the hierarchy under all operator stages generated
by trees definable in the same W-language.

5

The proposed number

Construction 5.1 (Contender 7). Define
A = OpStage(R, Index),
F = A(P5),
C7 = F (42, 42).
The complete definition layer is only:
def autonomousTransformer : Transformer :=
operatorStage reflectionTransformer index
def autonomousFamily : Family :=
autonomousTransformer p5Family
def contender_7 : Nat := autonomousFamily 42 42

The diagonal coordinate (42, 42) is deliberate. The first 42 exposes the outer transformer’s output
channel, while the second 42 is the ordinary numerical input and the visibility bound used by progressivity.
No occurrence of the constant contender_6 appears in these definitions.

5.1

Autonomous operator reflection

Computability in principle

Every component is executable:
• program enumerations at a fixed depth are finite lists;
• the interpreter of the finite-type W-language is total;
• reflect computes finite maxima and arithmetic;
• runTransformer and operatorStage recurse structurally on inductive W-trees;
• index is a closed W-tree whose branches are generated by total functions; and
• the seed p5Family is already a total function in the repository.
Consequently contender_7 is a closed Nat. “Computable in principle” does not mean feasible: evaluating
it would require an inconceivable amount of computation and must not be attempted. Lean checks its
definition and proofs symbolically, without normalizing the numeral.

6

Direct comparison without monotonicity

The key technical risk is monotonicity. A program in the object language can inspect an oracle value
and branch on it; increasing an oracle pointwise can therefore decrease a program’s output. Neither
reflect nor the lifted operators should be treated as monotone in their oracle argument. The proof
instead records explicit occurrences.

6.1

Progressive transformers

Definition 6.1 (Progressivity). A transformer T is progressive when
i≤n

=⇒

s(i, n) < T (s)(o, n)

for every seed s, every input n, and every output coordinate o.
The Lean predicate is
def Progressive (op : Transformer) : Prop :=
∀ (seed : Family) (i o n : Nat),
i ≤ n → seed i n < op seed o n

Ordinary reflection is progressive by the repository theorem oracle_lt_reflect.

6.2

Child dominance

At a node, a visible aligned child is literally one coordinate of the family supplied to the parent
transformer. Progressivity therefore gives the strict inequality directly.
Lemma 6.2 (Run child). If T is progressive and i ≤ n, then
Run(T, s, f (i))(i, n) < Run(T, s, node(f ))(o, n).
The corresponding Lean theorem is runTransformer_child_lt. No comparison between two
different oracle families is made.

A transformer lies below its autonomous lift

The universal index has the form
Index = node(k 7→ treesOfDepth(k)).
At depth zero, the term enumeration is empty. Every defaulted child of treesOfDepth 0 is therefore a
leaf. For any visible coordinate i ≤ n, there is an explicit two-edge path
leaf −→ treesOfDepth(0) −→ Index.
Applying the run-child lemma on each edge yields:
Theorem 6.3 (Lift dominance). If T is progressive and i ≤ n, then
T (s)(i, n) < Lift(T )(s)(o, n).
This is formalized as transformer_lt_lift_at. It immediately gives that autonomous lifting
preserves progressivity. Splicing also preserves progressivity when every selected child transformer is
progressive. A structural induction then proves:
Theorem 6.4 (Progressivity of operator stages). If the base transformer B is progressive, then
OpStage(B, a) is progressive for every W-tree a.
A second direct child lemma gives
OpStage(B, f (i))(s)(0, n) < OpStage(B, node(f ))(s)(o, n)

7

A stronger closure theorem

The definition is designed to exceed more than one named constant. Let t be any enumerated objectlanguage program denoting a W-tree. If t appears at finite enumeration depth k, then defTree t is
a child of treesOfDepth k, and that tree is a child of index. Reusing direct child dominance at the
operator level yields the theorem
theorem defined_operator_eventually_lt {k : Nat} {t : Term}
(ht : t ∈ BigNumber.allTerms k) :
∃ N, ∀ n, N ≤ n →
operatorStage reflectionTransformer (defTree t)
p5Family 0 n <
autonomousFamily n n

Thus every operator-stage whose outer schedule is denoted by a fixed program of the W-language is
eventually dominated by the autonomous family. This is the meaningful structural margin: Contender 7
closes not only over the old number, but over a whole class of independently generated operator
hierarchies.
The theorem is still deliberately modest. It does not claim that every total Lean transformer is
represented, nor does it quantify over arbitrary semantic well-orderings. Its scope is exactly the effective
W-language already audited by BusyLean.

Repository calibration

Let λ denote the proof-theoretic scale of the finite-type W-language. The repository informally calibrates
it near
λ ≈ ψ(Ωω ).
One reflection step relative to an oracle is treated as adding about λ to the fast-growing index. Iteration
along a W-tree of height α then has the rough effect of right multiplication by α. Because the universal
index ranges over the language’s own definable trees, its effective height is heuristically λ. On that
account:
C6 has scale roughly fλ·λ (42) = fλ2 (42).

Remark 8.1 (Limits of the calibration). The equation “Contender 7 is fλλ (42)” is not a theorem of this

in the required sense. The present Lean development proves strict numerical inequalities and structural
domination theorems, not ordinal equivalences.

Why not claim the System F jump yet?

The existing BusyLean article identifies impredicative polymorphism as a promising qualitative next
step. A fully satisfactory System F contender would require considerably more infrastructure:
1. syntax and decidable typing for a suitable presentation of System F;
2. substitution, reduction, and a deterministic normalizer;
3. a formal strong-normalization proof, most naturally through reducibility candidates in Lean’s
impredicative Prop;
4. a verified decoder for Church numerals;
5. a finite bounded syntax enumeration; and
6. an oracle extension whose normalization proof remains constructive.
Such a development could plausibly dwarf the predicative W-language, but an uncompiled sketch would
not satisfy the contest. The present proposal instead makes a smaller foundational change that can be
expressed entirely using the already audited W-language and structural recursion. It is intended as a
credible, reviewable Contender 7 rather than a promise of a stronger but unfinished system.

10.2

Excluded constructs

A source audit checks the Lean code, after removing comments, for the forbidden patterns
noncomputable, partial, unsafe, opaque, implemented_by, extern, native_decide, sorry, admit,
and custom axiom declarations.
It also checks that the definition layer does not mention contender_6; that expected declarations
exist; and that the aggregate BusyLean.lean imports all new modules. The Lean-side audit goes
further: it reconstructs the transitive declaration closure from the kernel environment and is set to
reject dependencies on the named Contender 6 and Contender 5 constants or selectors. That closure
check becomes authoritative only when the supplied test is run by Lean.

Avoiding accidental normalization

The comparison proof first proves a general pointwise inequality in a symbolic variable n, and only
then specializes to 42. The proof never unfolds the astronomical arithmetic computation. Rewriting is
directed through liftReflection_eq_stage, so elaboration needs only definitional unfolding of the
construction, not evaluation of the number.

10.4

Expected axiom profile

The mathematical proof uses direct natural-number inequalities, structural induction, function extensionality in the compatibility lemma, and existing BusyLean results. On Lean 4.34.0, the repository
permits propext and Quot.sound; the audit script rejects any reported axiom outside that set. The
exact reported set must be established by the final kernel run.

Likely integration failure modes

The most plausible issues are engineering rather than mathematical:
• Lean may require a small elaboration adjustment in a higher-order structural recursion equation;
• a rewrite may need an explicit unfolding directive to prevent the elaborator from choosing an
undesirable normal form;
• the toolchain may attribute propext or Quot.sound through core lemmas, as already happens
elsewhere in the repository; or
• clean-build time may exceed the contest budget despite short source.
None should be concealed. If the kernel rejects a term, the theorem is not yet established, regardless of
the paper proof. The validation report in the bundle records this distinction.

Further research questions

1. Kernel stabilization. Compile on Lean 4.34.0, minimize any elaboration fixes, and preserve the
definition/proof timing split.
2. Richer dependency-closure reporting. After the bundled rejection test passes, list and classify
all inherited constants under the old-language and proposal namespaces, as the Contender 6 audit
does.
3. Tighter structural theorem. Formalize domination of every finite iteration of liftTransformer,
perhaps using canonical spine trees and an explicit embedding into index.
4. Representation theorem. Characterize which progressive transformers can be represented by
W-language programs or by operatorStage schedules.
5. Ordinal lower bound. Define an ordinal notation semantics for the operator hierarchy and prove
a lower bound approaching λλ , rather than relying on the present calibration heuristic.

6. Ordinal upper bound. Show that the recursion does not silently exceed the proposed calibration
because of higher-type oracle routing. A matching upper bound would make the ordinal claim
meaningful.
7. Higher transformer levels. Iterate the construction at types F → F, (F → F) → (F → F), and

8. Diagonal autonomous tower. Encode all finite transformer levels in one inductive universe of

requires careful typing.
9. Impredicative successor. Complete a constructive System F normalization development, then
compare a bounded-depth System F maximum against Contender 7 without invoking classical choice.
10. Calculus of Constructions. Investigate a Lean-hosted, independently normalized dependent type

11. Complexity of checking. Measure elaboration and kernel costs separately. A definition may be
mathematically compact yet expensive for Lean because of generated recursors or closure size.
12. Robustness under toolchain upgrades. Test the axiom profile and generated code on subsequent

Conclusion

Contender 7 replaces a one-level autonomous reflection hierarchy by an autonomous hierarchy of
reflection operators. The proposal remains a closed, structurally recursive Lean natural number and
does not mention the previous contender constant. Its formal comparison strategy is deliberately
conservative: define progressivity, follow explicit child occurrences, identify the outer leaf with the
Contender 6 family, and compose two strict inequalities. The same argument proves eventual domination
of every operator-stage generated by a fixed W-language-definable tree.
The construction is therefore substantially more than an arithmetic wrapper. Under the repository’s
own proof-theoretic heuristic it moves from λ2 to λλ , with λ ≈ ψ(Ωω ). That estimate is not passed
off as a formal theorem. The formal deliverable is the Lean source for contender_7, its pointwise
comparison theorem, and the associated closure lemmas. The remaining gate is unambiguous: apply
the patch to the recorded repository revision and pass the supplied Lean 4.34.0 clean-build, timing,
smoke, and axiom checks.
